\begin{enumerate}

  \item For each scenario below, \textbf{(i)} identify whether the appropriate test is a
        chi-square test for homogeneity or independence, \textbf{(ii)} explain your reasoning
        based on the study design, and \textbf{(iii)} write $H_0$ and $H_a$ in context.

        \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]

          \item Three brands of coffee (Brand X, Y, Z) each recruited a random sample of
                50 coffee tasters who rated their experience as Prefer, Neutral, or Dislike.

                Test type: \ans{homogeneity}\gradedkey{\smash{\gradedblank[id=hw1a1, type=short, points=1, answer=homogeneity, check=text]{2.2cm}{0.2in}}}

                \boxguard[14]
                Reason: \ansline{Three separate samples (one per brand) each measure one categorical variable (taste rating).}\gradedkeyline{\gradedblank[id=hw1a2, type=rubric, points=1, display={\bfseries Separate samples, one variable.}]{\linewidth+6pt}{\gradedlinesheight{9}}}

                \boxguard[14]
                $H_0$: \ansline{The distribution of taste ratings (Prefer/Neutral/Dislike) is the same across Brands X, Y, and Z.}\gradedkeyline{\gradedblank[id=hw1a3, type=rubric, points=1, display={\bfseries Same for X, Y, Z.}]{\linewidth+6pt}{\gradedlinesheight{9}}}

                \boxguard[14]
                $H_a$: \ansline{The distribution of taste ratings is not the same for all three brands (at least one brand differs).}\gradedkeyline{\gradedblank[id=hw1a4, type=rubric, points=1, display={\bfseries Not all the same.}]{\linewidth+6pt}{\gradedlinesheight{9}}}

                \vspace{0.06in}

          \newpage
          \item A simple random sample of 400 shoppers is asked their age group (18--30,
                31--50, or 51+) and their preferred shopping method (In-store, Online, or Both).

                Test type: \ans{independence}\gradedkey{\smash{\gradedblank[id=hw1b1, type=short, points=1, answer=independence, check=text]{2.2cm}{0.2in}}}

                \boxguard[14]
                Reason: \ansline{One random sample measures two categorical variables (age group and shopping method) on each individual.}\gradedkeyline{\gradedblank[id=hw1b2, type=rubric, points=1, display={\bfseries One sample, two variables.}]{\linewidth+6pt}{\gradedlinesheight{9}}}

                \boxguard[14]
                $H_0$: \ansline{There is no association between age group and preferred shopping method in the shopper population.}\gradedkeyline{\gradedblank[id=hw1b3, type=rubric, points=1, display={\bfseries No association.}]{\linewidth+6pt}{\gradedlinesheight{9}}}

                \boxguard[14]
                $H_a$: \ansline{There is an association between age group and preferred shopping method in the shopper population.}\gradedkeyline{\gradedblank[id=hw1b4, type=rubric, points=1, display={\bfseries Association.}]{\linewidth+6pt}{\gradedlinesheight{9}}}

                \vspace{0.06in}

          \newpage
          \item A simple random sample of 200 high school athletes is classified by sport
                (Football, Basketball, or Soccer) and GPA level (3.0 and above, or Below 3.0).

                Test type: \ans{independence}\gradedkey{\smash{\gradedblank[id=hw1c1, type=short, points=1, answer=independence, check=text]{2.2cm}{0.2in}}}

                \boxguard[14]
                Reason: \ansline{One random sample measures two categorical variables (sport and GPA level) on each individual.}\gradedkeyline{\gradedblank[id=hw1c2, type=rubric, points=1, display={\bfseries One sample, two variables.}]{\linewidth+6pt}{\gradedlinesheight{9}}}

                \boxguard[14]
                $H_0$: \ansline{There is no association between sport and GPA level among high school athletes.}\gradedkeyline{\gradedblank[id=hw1c3, type=rubric, points=1, display={\bfseries No association.}]{\linewidth+6pt}{\gradedlinesheight{9}}}

                \boxguard[14]
                $H_a$: \ansline{There is an association between sport and GPA level among high school athletes.}\gradedkeyline{\gradedblank[id=hw1c4, type=rubric, points=1, display={\bfseries Association.}]{\linewidth+6pt}{\gradedlinesheight{9}}}

        \end{enumerate}

  \newpage
  \item A student says scenarios (b) and (c) in Problem 1 both involve two categorical
        variables, so both must be tests for independence. Is the student correct?
        Explain your reasoning.

        \ansline{Partially correct. Both scenarios do involve two categorical variables, and both are correctly classified as tests for independence --- so the student's conclusion happens to be right in this case. However, the student's reasoning is incomplete: the presence of two categorical variables alone does not determine the test type. The key factor is the study design: if one random sample is drawn and two variables are measured on each individual, use a test for independence; if separate samples are drawn from multiple groups and one variable is measured, use a test for homogeneity. The student should focus on the sampling structure, not just the number of variables.}\gradedkeyline{\gradedblank[id=hw2, type=rubric, points=2, display={Conclusion right (both are independence), reasoning wrong: two variables do not decide it --- the design does (one sample, two variables = independence; separate samples, one variable = homogeneity).}]{\linewidth+6pt}{\gradedlinesheight{4}}}

  \item A researcher randomly selects 50 students from each of 3 schools
        (School P, Q, R) and asks each student which after-school program they prefer
        (Academic Support, Sports, or Arts). Total $n = 150$.

        \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
          \item Identify the test type and write the hypotheses.

                \ansline{Test for homogeneity. $H_0$: The distribution of after-school program preference is the same across Schools P, Q, and R. $H_a$: The distribution of after-school program preference is not the same for all three schools.}\gradedkeyline{\gradedblank[id=hw3a, type=rubric, points=3, display={Homogeneity. $H_0$: program-preference distribution is the same for Schools P, Q, R. $H_a$: not the same for all three.}]{\linewidth+6pt}{\gradedlinesheight{3}}}

          \newpage
          \item Write out the two conditions for this chi-square test.
                Can either condition be verified without seeing the actual data table?
                Explain.

                \ansline{(1) Independence condition: data come from a stratified random sample (50 students randomly selected from each school); if sampling without replacement, need $50 \le 10\%$ of each school's population (requires each school to have at least 500 students --- cannot verify without knowing school sizes). (2) Large-counts condition: all expected counts must exceed 5 --- cannot be verified without the data table, since expected counts depend on the observed row and column totals.}\gradedkeyline{\gradedblank[id=hw3b, type=rubric, points=3, display={Random/10\%: 50 random students per school; need each school $\ge 500$ students (needs school sizes). Large counts: all expected counts $\ge 5$ --- needs the table. Neither can be verified yet.}]{\linewidth+6pt}{\gradedlinesheight{4}}}
        \end{enumerate}

  \item \textbf{AP-Style Multiple Choice.} Which of the following scenarios calls for
        a chi-square test for \textbf{homogeneity}?

        \begin{enumerate}[label=(\Alph*)]
          \item A random sample of 500 adults is asked their employment status
                (Employed/Unemployed) and their level of financial stress (Low/Medium/High).
          \item Researchers randomly select 80 men and 80 women and record each person's
                preferred exercise type (Cardio/Strength/Flexibility).
                \textcolor{keyred}{\textbf{$\leftarrow$ correct}}
          \item A random sample of 200 teenagers is asked their screen-time category
                ($<$2 hrs/$2$--$4$ hrs/$>$4 hrs/day) and their sleep quality (Good/Poor).
          \item A SRS of 300 patients from one hospital is classified by diagnosis
                (Diabetes/Hypertension/Neither) and insurance type (Private/Public/None).
                      \par\nobreak\rule{0pt}{0.45in}\hfill\gradedright{\textit{Letter:}~\gradedblank[id=hw4, type=choice, points=1, answer=B]{0.4in}{0.2in}}
        \end{enumerate}

        \begin{teachernote}
            (B) is correct: two separate random samples (men and women) each measure
            one categorical variable (exercise type) --- test for homogeneity.
            (A), (C), (D) all use one random sample and measure two variables --- tests
            for independence.
        \end{teachernote}

\end{enumerate}

\begin{reflectionbox}
In your own words, what is the single most important question to ask when deciding whether
to use a chi-square test for homogeneity or independence? Write 1--2 sentences.

\ansline{The most important question is: were the data collected from one random sample or from multiple separate groups? If multiple groups were sampled, use a test for homogeneity; if one sample was drawn and two variables were recorded on each person, use a test for independence.}
\end{reflectionbox}
