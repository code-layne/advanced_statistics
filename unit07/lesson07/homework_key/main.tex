\item \textbf{Response Times (AP FRQ Context).} \quad
A study compared mean emergency response times (minutes) between northern and southern
districts of a city. A 95\% CI for $\mu_{\text{northern}} - \mu_{\text{southern}}$ is
$(-2.37,\; 0.37)$ minutes.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*.]
  \boxguard
  \item Interpret the interval in context.
        \ansline{We are 95\% confident that the true difference in mean emergency response time (northern $-$ southern) for all calls in these districts is between $-2.37$ and $0.37$ minutes.}\gradedkeyline{\gradedblank[id=hw1a, type=rubric, points=2, display={\bfseries\boldmath 95\% confident $\mu_N - \mu_S$ (mean response time) is in $(-2.37, 0.37)$ min.}]{\linewidth+6pt}{\gradedlinesheight{1}}}
  \boxguard
  \item A city official claims northern response times are faster (lower mean) than
        southern. Does the interval support this claim? Explain.
        \ansline{No. 0 is inside the interval, so a difference of 0 is plausible. There is insufficient evidence to conclude that northern response times are faster.}\gradedkeyline{\gradedblank[id=hw1b, type=rubric, points=2, display={\bfseries\boldmath No: 0 is in the interval, so no difference is plausible.}]{\linewidth+6pt}{\gradedlinesheight{1}}}
  \boxguard
  \item What does it mean that this is a \emph{95\%} confidence interval? Describe in
        terms of repeated sampling.
        \ansline{In repeated random sampling with the same sample sizes, approximately 95\% of CIs constructed this way would capture the true difference $\mu_{\text{northern}} - \mu_{\text{southern}}$.}\gradedkeyline{\gradedblank[id=hw1c, type=rubric, points=2, display={\bfseries\boldmath About 95\% of intervals from repeated samples capture $\mu_N - \mu_S$.}]{\linewidth+6pt}{0.17in}}
\end{enumerate}

\item \textbf{Textbook Reading Levels.} \quad
A 90\% CI for $\mu_{\text{digital}} - \mu_{\text{print}}$ (mean reading comprehension score,
out of 100) is $(3.8,\; 9.2)$ points.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*.]
  \boxguard
  \item Interpret the interval in context.
        \ansline{We are 90\% confident that the true difference in mean reading comprehension score (digital $-$ print) for all students in these populations is between $3.8$ and $9.2$ points.}\gradedkeyline{\gradedblank[id=hw2a, type=rubric, points=2, display={\bfseries\boldmath 90\% confident $\mu_D - \mu_P$ (mean score) is in (3.8, 9.2) points.}]{\linewidth+6pt}{\gradedlinesheight{1}}}
  \boxguard
  \item A researcher claims that digital readers score higher on average. Does the CI
        provide convincing evidence? Explain.
        \ansline{Yes. The entire interval is positive (above 0), so 0 is not plausible. This provides convincing evidence that digital readers score higher on average.}\gradedkeyline{\gradedblank[id=hw2b, type=rubric, points=2, display={\bfseries\boldmath Yes: the whole interval is above 0; digital mean is higher.}]{\linewidth+6pt}{\gradedlinesheight{1}}}
\end{enumerate}

\item \textbf{Effect of Sample Size.} \quad
Two research teams study the same populations with the same standard deviations
($s_1 = s_2 = 8$ points). Team A uses $n_1 = n_2 = 25$; Team B uses $n_1 = n_2 = 100$.
Both use 95\% confidence.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*.]
  \item Compute the $SE$ for each team.

        Team A: $SE_A = \sqrt{\dfrac{8^2}{25} + \dfrac{8^2}{25}} = $ \ans{$\approx 2.263$ pts}\gradedkey{\gradedblank[id=hw3a1, type=short, points=1, answer=2.263, check=numeric, tolerance=0.005, display={\boldmath$2.263$}]{2.8cm}{0.19in}}

        Team B: $SE_B = \sqrt{\dfrac{8^2}{100} + \dfrac{8^2}{100}} = $ \ans{$\approx 1.131$ pts}\gradedkey{\gradedblank[id=hw3a2, type=short, points=1, answer=1.131, check=numeric, tolerance=0.005, display={\boldmath$1.131$}]{2.8cm}{0.19in}}

  \item Approximate the margin of error for each team, using $t^* \approx 2.0$.

        Team A: ME $\approx$ \ans{4.53 pts} \qquad Team B: ME $\approx$ \ans{2.26 pts}\gradedkey{\gradedblank[id=hw3b1, type=short, points=1, answer=4.53, check=numeric, tolerance=0.02, display={\boldmath$4.53$}]{2.8cm}{0.19in}\quad \gradedblank[id=hw3b2, type=short, points=1, answer=2.26, check=numeric, tolerance=0.02, display={\boldmath$2.26$}]{2.8cm}{0.19in}}

  \item By what factor is Team B's interval narrower than Team A's?
        \ansline{Team B's interval is $\approx 2\times$ narrower (factor of $\sqrt{4} = 2$), because sample sizes quadrupled.}\gradedkey{\gradedblank[id=hw3c, type=short, points=1, answer=2, check=numeric, tolerance=0, display={\boldmath$2$ (B's is half as wide)}]{2.3in}{0.19in}}
\end{enumerate}

\item \textbf{Comparing Weight-Loss Programs.} \quad
A 99\% CI for $\mu_{\text{program A}} - \mu_{\text{program B}}$ (mean weight loss, kg) is
$(-1.2,\; 4.8)$ kg.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*.]
  \boxguard
  \item Is there convincing evidence that one program produces greater mean weight loss?
        Explain using the interval.
        \ansline{No. 0 is inside the interval $(-1.2, 4.8)$, so a difference of 0 is plausible; insufficient evidence that the programs differ in mean weight loss.}\gradedkeyline{\gradedblank[id=hw4a, type=rubric, points=2, display={\bfseries\boldmath No: 0 is in $(-1.2, 4.8)$, so no difference is plausible.}]{\linewidth+6pt}{\gradedlinesheight{1}}}
  \boxguard
  \item If the confidence level were lowered to 90\% (all else equal), would the interval
        be narrower or wider? Explain.
        \ansline{Narrower. A lower confidence level requires a smaller $t^*$, which reduces the margin of error.}\gradedkeyline{\gradedblank[id=hw4b, type=rubric, points=2, display={\bfseries\boldmath Narrower: smaller $t^*$, so a smaller margin of error.}]{\linewidth+6pt}{\gradedlinesheight{1}}}
\end{enumerate}

\end{enumerate}

\begin{extensionbox}
A 95\% CI for $\mu_1 - \mu_2$ excludes 0. Must a two-sided significance test for
$H_0: \mu_1 = \mu_2$ at $\alpha = 0.05$ also reject $H_0$? Explain the connection
between confidence intervals and significance tests.
\ansline{Yes. A 95\% CI and a two-sided test at $\alpha = 0.05$ are mathematically equivalent: if 0 is outside the CI, then the $p$-value for the two-sided test is less than 0.05, so $H_0$ is rejected. The CI captures all null values that would not be rejected at that level.}
\end{extensionbox}
