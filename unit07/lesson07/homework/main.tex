\item \textbf{Response Times (AP FRQ Context).} \quad
A study compared mean emergency response times (minutes) between northern and southern
districts of a city. A 95\% CI for $\mu_{\text{northern}} - \mu_{\text{southern}}$ is
$(-2.37,\; 0.37)$ minutes.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*.]
  \boxguard
  \item Interpret the interval in context. \gradedhide{\writeline\\\gradedhere{\gradedblank[id=hw1a, type=rubric, points=2, display={\bfseries\boldmath 95\% confident $\mu_N - \mu_S$ (mean response time) is in $(-2.37, 0.37)$ min.}]{\linewidth+6pt}{\gradedlinesheight{1}}}\writelines{9}}
  \boxguard
  \item A city official claims northern response times are faster (lower mean) than
        southern. Does the interval support this claim? Explain. \gradedhide{\writeline\\\gradedhere{\gradedblank[id=hw1b, type=rubric, points=2, display={\bfseries\boldmath No: 0 is in the interval, so no difference is plausible.}]{\linewidth+6pt}{\gradedlinesheight{1}}}\writelines{9}}
  \boxguard
  \item What does it mean that this is a \emph{95\%} confidence interval? Describe in
        terms of repeated sampling. \gradedhide{\writeline}\gradedbelow[l]{1.5pt}{\kern-3pt\gradedblank[id=hw1c, type=rubric, points=2, display={\bfseries\boldmath About 95\% of intervals from repeated samples capture $\mu_N - \mu_S$.}]{\linewidth+6pt}{0.17in}}\par\vspace{2.05in}
\end{enumerate}

\item \textbf{Effect of Sample Size.} \quad
Two research teams study the same populations with the same standard deviations
($s_1 = s_2 = 8$ points). Team A uses $n_1 = n_2 = 25$; Team B uses $n_1 = n_2 = 100$.
Both use 95\% confidence.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*.]
  \item Compute the $SE$ for each team.

        Team A: $SE_A = \sqrt{\dfrac{8^2}{25} + \dfrac{8^2}{25}} = $ \makebox[3cm]{\smash{\gradedblank[id=hw3a1, type=short, points=1, answer=2.263, check=numeric, tolerance=0.005, display={\boldmath$2.263$}]{2.8cm}{0.19in}}}

        Team B: $SE_B = \sqrt{\dfrac{8^2}{100} + \dfrac{8^2}{100}} = $ \makebox[3cm]{\smash{\gradedblank[id=hw3a2, type=short, points=1, answer=1.131, check=numeric, tolerance=0.005, display={\boldmath$1.131$}]{2.8cm}{0.19in}}}

  \item Approximate the margin of error for each team, using $t^* \approx 2.0$.

        Team A: ME $\approx$ \makebox[3cm]{\smash{\gradedblank[id=hw3b1, type=short, points=1, answer=4.53, check=numeric, tolerance=0.02, display={\boldmath$4.53$}]{2.8cm}{0.19in}}} \qquad Team B: ME $\approx$ \makebox[3cm]{\smash{\gradedblank[id=hw3b2, type=short, points=1, answer=2.26, check=numeric, tolerance=0.02, display={\boldmath$2.26$}]{2.8cm}{0.19in}}}

  \item By what factor is Team B's interval narrower than Team A's? \gradedhide{\writeline}\gradedright{\gradedblank[id=hw3c, type=short, points=1, answer=2, check=numeric, tolerance=0, display={\boldmath$2$ (B's is half as wide)}]{2.3in}{0.19in}}
\end{enumerate}

\item \textbf{Comparing Weight-Loss Programs.} \quad
A 99\% CI for $\mu_{\text{program A}} - \mu_{\text{program B}}$ (mean weight loss, kg) is
$(-1.2,\; 4.8)$ kg.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*.]
  \boxguard
  \item Is there convincing evidence that one program produces greater mean weight loss?
        Explain using the interval. \gradedhide{\writeline\\\gradedhere{\gradedblank[id=hw4a, type=rubric, points=2, display={\bfseries\boldmath No: 0 is in $(-1.2, 4.8)$, so no difference is plausible.}]{\linewidth+6pt}{\gradedlinesheight{1}}}\writelines{9}}
  \boxguard
  \item If the confidence level were lowered to 90\% (all else equal), would the interval
        be narrower or wider? Explain. \gradedhide{\writeline\\\gradedhere{\gradedblank[id=hw4b, type=rubric, points=2, display={\bfseries\boldmath Narrower: smaller $t^*$, so a smaller margin of error.}]{\linewidth+6pt}{\gradedlinesheight{1}}}\writelines{9}}
\end{enumerate}

\end{enumerate}

\begin{extensionbox}
A 95\% CI for $\mu_1 - \mu_2$ excludes 0. Must a two-sided significance test for
$H_0: \mu_1 = \mu_2$ at $\alpha = 0.05$ also reject $H_0$? Explain the connection
between confidence intervals and significance tests.
\writelines{3}
\end{extensionbox}
