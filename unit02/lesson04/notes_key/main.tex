\termans{Least-squares regression line}{the line $\hat y = a + bx$ that makes the sum of the squared residuals as small as possible.}

\begin{guidednotes}
% ── ROW 1: the least-squares line from summary statistics ────────────────────
\mainidea[The best line:]{Least squares} &
The \textbf{least-squares regression line (LSRL)} is the one line that makes the sum of the
\ans{squared} residuals as small as possible. It always passes through \ans{$(\bar x, \bar y)$}.

\vspace{2pt}
Twenty students: \textbf{sleep} $x$ (hours, 4 to 10) and \textbf{reaction time} $y$ (ms).
\ $\bar x = 7.2$, \ $s_x = 1.5$, \ $\bar y = 310$, \ $s_y = 45$, \ $r = -0.80$.
\par\vspace{2pt}
\stepnum{1}\ Slope: $b = r \cdot s_y / s_x$ --- always the same \ans{sign} as $r$.\par
\stepnum{2}\ Intercept: $a = \bar y - b\,\bar x$, because the line goes through
$(\bar x, \bar y)$.\par
\stepnum{3}\ Write $\hat y = a + bx$ with the \ans{variable names} in place of $x$ and $y$.
\notesprompt{Build the sleep line.}
\begin{probgrid}{|Y|Y|Y|}
\pcell{1}{The slope $b$}{1.3cm}{$-0.80\cdot\frac{45}{1.5} = -24$ ms per hour} &
\pcell{2}{The intercept $a$}{1.3cm}{$310 - (-24)(7.2)$\par $= 482.8$ ms} &
\pcell{3}{The equation, with the variable names}{1.3cm}{$\widehat{\text{time}} = 482.8$\par $\;- 24(\text{sleep})$} \\ \hline
\end{probgrid}
\\ \hline

% ── ROW 3: interpreting slope and intercept — THE CRUX (two sub-rows) ────────
\mainidea[In context:]{Slope \& intercept} &
The \textbf{slope} $b$ is how much the \ans{predicted} $y$ changes for each \ans{1-unit}
increase in $x$ --- a fact about the line, not a promise about every individual. The
\textbf{intercept} $a$ is the predicted $y$ when $x =$ \ans{0}; it means something only
if $x = 0$ makes sense \emph{and} is \ans{near the data}.
\par\vspace{3pt}
\fcolorbox{navy}{sky}{\parbox{\dimexpr\linewidth-2\fboxsep-2\fboxrule\relax}{\centering
\textbf{For each additional [unit of $x$], the \emph{predicted} [$y$] increases (decreases)
by [$|b|$ units].}}}
\\
& \notesprompt{Interpret the sleep line, $\widehat{\text{time}} = 482.8 - 24(\text{sleep})$.}
\begin{probgrid}{|Y|Y|}
\pcell{7}{Interpret the slope in context.}{1.7cm}{For each extra hour of sleep, the \emph{predicted} reaction time drops by 24 ms.} &
\pcell{8}{Interpret the intercept. Is it meaningful here?}{1.7cm}{482.8 ms predicted at 0 hr of sleep. Not meaningful: 0 is far outside 4--10 hr.} \\ \hline
\pcell{9}{Jon slept 2 hours more than Ana. How do their \emph{predicted} times
compare?}{1.7cm}{Jon's predicted time is $2 \times 24 = 48$ ms lower than Ana's.} &
\pcell{10}{Maya writes: ``When a student sleeps one more hour, their reaction time goes down
24~ms.'' What is wrong?}{1.7cm}{Say \emph{predicted}: real times scatter around the line --- and this shows no cause.} \\ \hline
\end{probgrid}
