{\color{navy}\textbf{TWO-WAY TABLES}}\par
\textbf{Marginal:} a row or column total $\div$ the grand total.\par
\textbf{Joint:} one cell $\div$ the grand total.\par
\textbf{Conditional:} one cell $\div$ the total of the \emph{given} group.
``Of the walk-ins, \ldots'' $\Rightarrow$ divide by the walk-in total.\par
\textbf{Association:} the conditional distributions \emph{differ} across groups.
Compare the \emph{same} percent for each group --- percents, not counts.\par
\textbf{Association is not causation} --- observational data cannot show cause.\par\vspace{4pt}
{\color{navy}\textbf{SCATTERPLOTS}}\par
\textbf{Explanatory} variable on $x$ (predicts); \textbf{response} on $y$.\par
\textbf{Describe:} direction (positive / negative), form (linear / curved),
strength (strong / moderate / weak), and unusual points --- \emph{in context}.\par\vspace{4pt}
{\color{navy}\textbf{CORRELATION $r$}}\par
$-1 \le r \le 1$: sign = direction, size = strength. \textbf{Linear} association only;
a high $r$ does \emph{not} prove a line fits.\par
No units; unchanged by new units or by swapping $x$ and $y$. Not resistant to unusual points.
\end{minipage}\hfill
\begin{minipage}[t]{0.485\linewidth}\raggedright
{\color{navy}\textbf{LEAST-SQUARES REGRESSION LINE}}\par
$\hat{y} = a + bx$ \qquad $b = r\,\dfrac{s_y}{s_x}$ \qquad $a = \bar{y} - b\bar{x}$\par
The line always passes through $(\bar{x}, \bar{y})$.\par
\textbf{Computer output:} \texttt{Constant} Coef $= a$; the $x$-variable's Coef $= b$;
\texttt{R-Sq} $= r^2$; \texttt{S} $= s$.\par
\textbf{Residual} $= y - \hat{y}$ (actual $-$ predicted). Positive: the point is above the
line, so the line \emph{under}-predicted.\par\vspace{4pt}
{\color{navy}\textbf{SENTENCE FRAMES}}\par
\textbf{Slope:} For each additional [unit] of [$x$], the \emph{predicted} [$y$]
increases / decreases by [$b$] [units].\par
\textbf{Intercept:} When [$x$] is 0, the predicted [$y$] is [$a$].\par
\textbf{$r^2$:} About [\%] of the variation in [$y$] is explained by the linear model
with [$x$].\par
\textbf{$s$:} Actual [$y$] values are typically about [$s$] [units] from the predicted values.\par\vspace{4pt}
{\color{navy}\textbf{IS A LINE APPROPRIATE?}}\par
\textbf{Residual plot:} random scatter around 0 $\Rightarrow$ a line fits.
A curve $\Rightarrow$ a line does not fit, whatever $r$ is.\par
\textbf{Extrapolation:} predicting outside the data's $x$-range is unreliable.\par
\textbf{Outlier:} a large residual. \textbf{High leverage:} an $x$ far from the rest.
\textbf{Influential:} removing it changes the line a lot.
\end{minipage}}
