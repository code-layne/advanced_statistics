\small
For each experiment, identify units, factor(s), treatments, response variable, design
type, and blinding used.

\vspace{8pt}
\textbf{1.} A study tests whether a new sleep aid improves sleep quality. 100 adults
are randomly assigned: 50 take the pill nightly; 50 take a look-alike sugar pill.
Participants don't know which pill they take. A sleep technician, also unaware of
treatment, rates sleep quality.

\vspace{4pt}
Experimental units: \makebox[8cm][l]{\vphantom{\blank{8cm}}\hspace{46bp}\smash{\gradedblank[id=hw1a, type=short, points=1, answer={100 adults}, check=text]{2.4in}{0.17in}}}\\[4pt]
Factor: \makebox[8cm][l]{\vphantom{\blank{8cm}}\hspace{46bp}\smash{\gradedblank[id=hw1b, type=short, points=1, answer={type of sleep aid}, check=text]{2.4in}{0.17in}}}\\[4pt]
Treatments: \makebox[8cm][l]{\vphantom{\blank{8cm}}\hspace{46bp}\smash{\gradedblank[id=hw1c, type=rubric, points=1, display={\bfseries new pill; sugar pill}]{2.6in}{0.17in}}}\\[4pt]
Response variable: \makebox[8cm][l]{\vphantom{\blank{8cm}}\hspace{46bp}\smash{\gradedblank[id=hw1d, type=short, points=1, answer={sleep quality rating}, check=text]{2.4in}{0.17in}}}\\[4pt]
Design type: \makebox[4cm][l]{\vphantom{\blank{4cm}}\hspace{44bp}\smash{\gradedblank[id=hw1e, type=short, points=1, answer={completely randomized design}, check=text, display={\bfseries CRD}]{1.05in}{0.17in}}} \quad Blinding: \makebox[4cm][l]{\vphantom{\blank{4cm}}\hspace{46bp}\smash{\gradedblank[id=hw1f, type=short, points=1, answer=double-blind, check=text]{1.4in}{0.17in}}}\\[4pt]
Why is the placebo important? \gradedhide{\writeline\gradedright{\gradedhere{\gradedblank[id=hw1g, type=rubric, points=2, display={Controls the placebo effect (believing you took a pill); isolates the drug's true effect.}]{4.2in}{\gradedlinesheight{2}}}\hspace{\dimexpr 4.2in-3pt\relax}}\\[1pt]\writeline}

\vspace{8pt}
\textbf{2.} A farmer wants to compare 3 fertilizers. He has 90 plants all grown in
the same greenhouse. He randomly assigns 30 plants to each fertilizer.

\vspace{4pt}
Design type: \makebox[4cm][l]{\vphantom{\blank{4cm}}\hspace{44bp}\smash{\gradedblank[id=hw2a, type=short, points=1, answer={completely randomized design}, check=text, display={\bfseries CRD}]{1.05in}{0.17in}}} \quad Blocking variable (if any): \makebox[4cm][l]{\vphantom{\blank{4cm}}\hspace{46bp}\smash{\gradedblank[id=hw2b, type=short, points=1, answer=none, check=text]{1.4in}{0.17in}}}\\[4pt]
Would adding blocks improve this design? Explain. \gradedhide{\writeline\gradedright{\gradedhere{\gradedblank[id=hw2c, type=rubric, points=2, display={No: same greenhouse, no nuisance variable to block on.}]{3in}{\gradedlinesheight{2}}}\hspace{\dimexpr 3in-3pt\relax}}\\[1pt]\writeline}

\vspace{8pt}
\textbf{3.} A researcher tests two diets on 60 participants. She pairs participants
by current weight (closest pairs); one in each pair gets Diet A, one gets Diet B
(randomly assigned).

\vspace{4pt}
Design type: \makebox[7cm][l]{\vphantom{\blank{7cm}}\hspace{46bp}\smash{\gradedblank[id=hw3a, type=short, points=1, answer={matched pairs design}, check=text]{2in}{0.17in}}}\\[4pt]
Why is pairing by weight appropriate here? \gradedhide{\writeline\gradedright{\gradedhere{\gradedblank[id=hw3b, type=rubric, points=2, display={Current weight affects weight loss; pairing removes it, so the diet comparison is more precise.}]{3.5in}{\gradedlinesheight{2}}}\hspace{\dimexpr 3.5in-3pt\relax}}\\[1pt]\writeline}

\vspace{8pt}
\textbf{4.} Reflection: Why does random \emph{assignment} (not just random selection)
allow us to conclude causation from an experiment?\\[4pt]
\gradedhide{\writeline\gradedright{\gradedhere{\gradedblank[id=hw4, type=rubric, points=2, display={Random assignment spreads confounders (motivation, health, habits) roughly evenly across groups, so a difference in response can be attributed to the treatment.}]{6.1in}{\gradedlinesheight{3}}}\hspace{\dimexpr 6.1in-3pt\relax}}\\[1pt]\writeline\\[1pt]\writeline}
