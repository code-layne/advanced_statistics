% ── ROW 1: could chance alone do this? ────────────────────────────────────────
\mainidea[Could chance do it?]{Significant} &
\textbf{Statistical inference} uses data to draw conclusions beyond the \blank{2.6cm} we
measured. In an experiment, even if the treatment does nothing, the luck of the random
\blank{2.0cm} makes the groups differ a little. A difference so large that it would
\blank{1.4cm} happen by chance alone is \textbf{statistically significant}.
\\
& \begin{minipage}[c]{0.44\linewidth}\raggedright
24 volunteers were randomly assigned to study a word list in silence or with music, 12 each.
The silence group recalled \textbf{2.5 more words} on average.\par\vspace{3pt}
A computer then reshuffled the 24 scores into two new groups of 12, \textbf{100 times}, as if
music made no difference.
\end{minipage}\hfill

% ── ROW 2: the scope of inference ─────────────────────────────────────────────
\mainidea[What can we say?]{Scope of inference} &
The \textbf{scope of inference} says what a study lets us conclude. Random \blank{2.4cm}
of treatments lets us conclude \textbf{cause and effect}. Random \blank{2.0cm} of the
individuals from a population lets us \textbf{generalize} to that \blank{2.2cm}.

% ── ROW 3: cause or only association — THE CRUX ───────────────────────────────
\mainidea[Association or]{Cause?} &
With no random assignment, a \blank{2.4cm} variable may explain an association, so we can
say only that the variables are \blank{2.2cm}. A statistically significant difference in an
experiment with random assignment is evidence that the treatment \blank{1.6cm} it.
\par\vspace{3pt}
A random sample of \textbf{500 teens} from across the state: teens who study with music have
lower GPAs, on average, than teens who study in silence.
\notesprompt{No one was assigned to anything.}
\begin{probgrid}{|Y|Y|Y|}
\pcell{8}{Can we say the association holds for all teens in the state? Why?}{2.2cm}{} &
\pcell{9}{Name a variable that could explain the lower GPAs instead of music.}{2.2cm}{} &
\pcell{10}{``The 500 were chosen at random, so this proves music lowers GPA.'' Agree? Why?}{2.2cm}{} \\ \hline
\end{probgrid}

% ── ROW 4: guided practice — WE DO, together, fresh context ──────────────────
\mainidea[Guided practice]{The Reading App} &
A district picks \textbf{40 ninth graders at random} from all of its ninth graders and randomly
assigns 20 to use a reading app for 10 minutes a day and 20 to no app. After 8 weeks the app
group's mean reading-score gain is \textbf{6 points higher}. In 100 random reshuffles of the 40
gains (as if the app did nothing), only \textbf{1} gave a difference of 6 points or more.
\notesprompt{We work these together.}
\begin{probgrid}{|Y|Y|}
\pcell{11}{Is the 6-point difference statistically significant? Why?}{2.0cm}{} &
\pcell{12}{Can we conclude the app caused the higher gains? Why?}{2.0cm}{} \\ \hline
\pcell{13}{To whom can we generalize the result? Why?}{2.0cm}{} &
\pcell{14}{A second district picks 200 ninth graders at random; those who already use the app score higher. ``Random sample, so the app works.'' Respond.}{2.0cm}{} \\ \hline
\end{probgrid}
