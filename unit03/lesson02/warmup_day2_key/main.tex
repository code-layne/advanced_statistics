\par\vspace{-6pt}\small\noindent
Camp Tall Pines has 400 campers: 100 in each of four age groups (8--9, 10--11, 12--13, 14--15).
They sleep in 25 cabins, and \textbf{every cabin mixes all four age groups}. The director wants
the mean amount campers spend at the camp store in a week.
\par\vspace{4pt}
\begin{probgrid}{|Y|Y|}
\pcell{1}{The campers are numbered 001--400. Use these digits, three at a time, to pick the
first three campers:\quad\texttt{382 017 455 382 096}}{1.6cm}{382, 017, 096 --- skip 455 (out of
range) and the second 382 (a repeat).} &
\pcell{2}{Randomly choose 3 of the 25 cabins and survey every camper in them. Name the
method.}{1.6cm}{Cluster sample: each cabin is a mix, like the whole camp.} \\ \hline
\pcell{3}{Randomly choose 10 campers from each of the four age groups. Name the
method.}{1.6cm}{Stratified random sample: the age groups are the strata.} &
\pcell{4}{Randomly choose one age group and survey all 100. Every camper has a 1-in-4 chance.
Is it an SRS? Why?}{1.6cm}{No: a sample that mixes age groups can never be chosen, so not every
group of 100 has an equal chance.} \\ \hline
\end{probgrid}
\par\vspace{2pt}
{\footnotesize\textbf{Think ahead:} older campers spend more at the store. Which plan, 2 or 3,
makes sure every age group is in the sample? \quad\ans{Plan 3, the stratified sample.}}
}
