\begin{notesbox}{Part A --- Name and Compare the Plans}
\interlinepenalty=10000 % keep each prompt whole when the box breaks
\small
Mercy Valley Hospital has 720 nurses. Each nurse works the day, evening, or night shift (240 on
each), and each of the hospital's 12 floors has 60 nurses: 20 from each shift. The
administrator wants to estimate the mean hours of sleep the nurses get before a work shift.
Night-shift nurses usually sleep much less than the others.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{A\arabic*.}, leftmargin=2.2em, itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Plan 1:} randomly choose 3 of the 12 floors and survey every nurse on those
        floors. Name the sampling method.
        \answerspace{0.42in}{\normalcolor\mdseries\vspace{2pt}\gradedblank[id=hw1, type=short, points=1, answer={cluster sample}, check=text, display={cluster sample}]{6cm}{0.3in}}
  \item \textbf{Plan 2:} take a simple random sample of 12 nurses from each of the three shifts.
        Name the sampling method.
        \answerspace{0.42in}{\normalcolor\mdseries\vspace{2pt}\gradedblank[id=hw2, type=short, points=1, answer={stratified random sample}, check=text, display={stratified random sample}]{6cm}{0.3in}}
  \item \textbf{AP-style.} Which plan is most likely to give the most precise estimate of the
        mean hours of sleep for all 720 nurses? Write its letter in the Letter box, then justify
        it in a sentence.
        \begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{(\Alph*)}, leftmargin=2.2em, itemsep=0pt]
          \item A simple random sample of 36 nurses from the hospital's list of all nurses.
          \item Plan 1: every nurse on 3 floors chosen at random.
          \item \ans{Plan 2: a simple random sample of 12 nurses from each shift.}
          \item The first 36 nurses who arrive on Monday morning.
          \item Every nurse on 3 floors the administrator chooses.
                \hfill\gradedright{\textit{Letter:}~\gradedblank[id=hw3, type=choice, points=1, answer=C]{0.45in}{0.24in}}\rule[-0.2in]{0pt}{0.48in}
        \end{enumerate}
        \answerspace{2.1in}{\normalcolor\mdseries\hspace*{10.5pt}\gradedblank[id=hw3j, type=rubric, points=1, display={(C): sleep is alike within a shift, so stratifying by shift guarantees night-shift nurses are included and the estimate varies less than an SRS of the same size.}]{\linewidth-10.5pt}{0.7cm}}
\end{enumerate}
\end{notesbox}

\boxguard[16]
\begin{notesbox}{Part B --- Plans in Practice}
\interlinepenalty=10000 % keep each prompt whole when the box breaks
\small
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{B\arabic*.}, leftmargin=2.2em, itemsep=2pt]
  \item Describe how to use a random number generator to choose a simple random sample of 36 of
        the 720 nurses.
        \answerspace{2.1in}{\normalcolor\mdseries\hspace*{10.5pt}\gradedblank[id=hw4, type=rubric, points=2, display={Number the nurses 1 to 720 on the hospital's list. Generate random integers from 1 to 720, skipping repeats, until there are 36 different numbers. Survey those 36 nurses.}]{\linewidth-10.5pt}{0.95cm}}
  \item Give one practical advantage of Plan 1 over Plan 2 for the person running the survey.
        \answerspace{2.1in}{\normalcolor\mdseries\hspace*{10.5pt}\gradedblank[id=hw5, type=rubric, points=2, display={Plan 1 is cheaper and easier: the surveyor visits only 3 floors and surveys everyone there, instead of finding 36 nurses spread over all 12 floors and three shifts.}]{\linewidth-10.5pt}{0.95cm}}
  \item A supervisor says, ``Use the shifts as clusters: randomly pick one shift and survey all
        240 nurses on it.'' Explain why this is a poor plan, and say how the shifts should be
        used instead.
        \answerspace{2.1in}{\normalcolor\mdseries\hspace*{10.5pt}\gradedblank[id=hw6, type=rubric, points=2, display={A shift is not like the whole hospital: if the night shift is picked the mean is far too low, and if not, night-shift nurses are left out. Shifts are alike inside, so use them as strata and sample from every shift.}]{\linewidth-10.5pt}{0.95cm}}
\end{enumerate}
\end{notesbox}
