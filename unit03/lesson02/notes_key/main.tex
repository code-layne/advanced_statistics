\begin{guidednotes}
% ── ROW 1: the simple random sample ──────────────────────────────────────────
\mainidea[Chance chooses]{SRS} &
In a \textbf{simple random sample (SRS)} of size $n$, every \ans{group} of $n$ individuals
has an \ans{equal} chance of being the sample. Sampling \textbf{without replacement}: an
individual can be chosen only \ans{once}. \textbf{With replacement}: an individual can be
chosen more than once.

\vspace{3pt}
\stepnum{1}\ \textbf{Label:} give each individual a different number, 1 to $N$.\par
\stepnum{2}\ \textbf{Randomize:} use a random number generator or a table of random digits.\par
\stepnum{3}\ \textbf{Select:} skip numbers out of range and \ans{repeats} until you have $n$.
\\
& Lakeview High has 1{,}200 students: 300 in each of grades 9--12. The principal wants the mean
number of hours per week Lakeview students work at a paid job.
\notesprompt{Choose an SRS of Lakeview students.}
\begin{probgrid}{|Y|Y|Y|}
\pcell{1}{Describe how to use a random number generator to choose an SRS of 80 students.}{2.8cm}{Number them 1--1{,}200. Generate random integers 1--1{,}200; skip repeats until 80 differ. Survey them.} &
\pcell{2}{Students are numbered 0001--1200. Use these digits, four at a time, to pick the first three students:\par
\texttt{0738 1195 9661 0738 0452}}{2.8cm}{738, 1195, 452 (skip 9661 and the repeat 0738).} &
\pcell{3}{A teacher draws a name from a hat of all 1{,}200, puts it back, then draws again. With or without replacement?}{2.8cm}{With replacement: a student could be picked twice.} \\ \hline
\end{probgrid}
\\ \hline
% ── ROW 2: strata and clusters — THE TRAP ────────────────────────────────────
\mainidea[Groups first]{Strata \& clusters} &
\textbf{Stratified random sample:} split the population into \textbf{strata}, groups whose
members are \ans{like} each other; take an \ans{SRS} from \ans{every} stratum and
combine them.\par\vspace{3pt}
\textbf{Cluster sample:} split the population into \textbf{clusters}, groups that each look like
a small copy of the \ans{population}; randomly choose some clusters and measure \ans{every}
individual in them.\par\vspace{3pt}
{\footnotesize (One more, named only: a \textbf{systematic random sample} picks a random starting
point on a list, then every $k$th individual.)}
\\
& \notesprompt{Lakeview's 48 advisory groups each mix students from all four grades. Name each method.}
\begin{probgrid}{|Y|Y|Y|}
\pcell{4}{Randomly choose 20 students from each of grades 9, 10, 11, and 12.}{1.4cm}{Stratified (grades are strata).} &
\pcell{5}{Randomly choose 2 of the 48 advisory groups; survey every student in them.}{1.4cm}{Cluster (groups are clusters).} &
\pcell{6}{Randomly choose 1 grade; survey all 300 in it. Each student has a 1-in-4 chance. Is it an SRS?}{1.4cm}{No: a mixed-grade group can never be chosen.} \\ \hline
\end{probgrid}
\\ \hline
% ── ROW 3: choosing a method — THE CRUX ──────────────────────────────────────
\mainidea[Which plan?]{Choosing} &
\textbf{Stratify} when the response \ans{differs} from group to group: strata alike inside give
an estimate that \ans{varies} less from sample to sample than an SRS of the same size.
\textbf{Cluster} when reaching scattered individuals is \ans{expensive}: it is cheaper and more
practical, and it works best when each cluster looks like the population.
\notesprompt{Seniors at Lakeview work far more hours than freshmen. Choose and defend a plan.}
\begin{probgrid}{|Y|Y|}
\pcell{7}{Why would 20 from each grade beat an SRS of 80 for estimating mean hours worked?}{1.6cm}{Hours are alike within a grade, so each grade is covered and the estimate varies less.} &
\pcell{8}{Give one advantage of the advisory-group cluster sample over the plan in problem~7.}{1.6cm}{Cheaper and easier: visit 2 rooms instead of finding 80 students all over the school.} \\ \hline
\pcell{9}{Should the principal use the 48 advisory groups as \emph{strata}? Why or why not?}{1.9cm}{No. Each group is a mix like the whole school, so stratifying by them gains nothing.} &
\pcell{10}{Jalen: ``Use the grades as clusters --- randomly pick 2 grades and survey everyone in them.'' What is wrong with his plan?}{1.9cm}{Grades are not like the whole school; 2 grades could miss the seniors. Use grades as strata.} \\ \hline
\end{probgrid}
