\boxguard[8]
\begin{remindbox}
\small
A sample can go wrong in three places: \textbf{who chose} to be in it (volunteers, or whoever
was handy), \textbf{who is missing} (never on the list, or picked but never answered), and
\textbf{how the question was asked}. Naming the bias is only half an answer --- say who is
over- or under-represented and whether the estimate is \textbf{too high or too low}, in
context. And size is no cure: \textbf{a larger sample reduces variability, not bias.}
\end{remindbox}
