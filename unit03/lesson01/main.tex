\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=sky, colframe=navy, boxrule=0.9pt, arc=2mm,
    left=2mm, right=2mm, top=1.5mm, bottom=1.5mm]
    \textbf{Primary Objective:} Students will classify a data collection method as chance-based
    or not, name the population and the sample in a study (and say what a census is),
    generalize a sample's result only to the population it was drawn from, and classify a study
    as an observational study or an experiment by asking whether a treatment was imposed ---
    rejecting the claim that careful measurement makes a study an experiment.
    \\[2pt]
    \textbf{CED alignment:} Topics 3.1 (Introducing Statistics: Do the Data We Collected Tell
    the Truth?) and 3.2 (Introduction to Planning a Study) \quad$\cdot$\quad Big Ideas: VAR
    (Variation and Distribution), DAT (Data Analysis) \quad$\cdot$\quad Skills 1.A, 1.C, 4.A
    \quad$\cdot$\quad LO VAR-1.E, DAT-2.A, DAT-2.B \quad$\cdot$\quad EK VAR-1.E.1, DAT-2.A.1--4,
    DAT-2.B.1--3; census from DAT-2.C.6 (named here, used again in Lesson 3.2)
    \\[2pt]
    \textbf{Lesson model:} \emph{Gradual release --- I do, we do, you do --- all of it inside
    the guided notes.} There is no group activity. The notes name the vocabulary as they teach
    it and deliver the instruction row by row in one \emph{Main Ideas / Notes} table --- each
    idea a definition to complete and a grid of short problems, the first worked by you and the
    rest by the class; the last row, Guided Practice, is worked together with students holding
    the pen; \textbf{the homework is the individual practice}, and students start it in class
    while you circulate. The whole-class debrief is \emph{spoken}.
    \\[2pt]
    \textbf{Compact budget:} the notes are two pages, 14 problems. Nothing is sketched; every
    study is a one- or two-sentence description, read and classified. Held back on purpose: the
    named sampling methods (3.2), the named biases and ``bigger is not better'' (3.3), the
    principles of experimental design (3.4), and the scope-of-inference grid (3.5).
\end{tcolorbox}

\boxguard
\begin{skillbox}[Learning Targets \& Key Understandings]{goldbox}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}X|X@{}}
\textbf{Learning targets (student language)} & \textbf{Key understandings (the ``why'')} \\[2pt]
\hline
\vspace{-6pt}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.1em, nosep, after=\vspace{2pt}]
  \item I can tell a chance-based method of collecting data from a non-chance method, and
        explain why only chance earns our trust.
  \item I can name the population and the sample in a study, say what a census is, and
        generalize only to the population that was sampled.
  \item I can classify a study as an observational study or an experiment by asking whether a
        treatment was imposed.
  \item I can explain why only a well-designed experiment can show cause and effect.
\end{itemize}
&
\vspace{-6pt}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.1em, nosep, after=\vspace{2pt}]
  \item Methods for data collection that do not rely on chance result in untrustworthy
        conclusions (VAR-1.E.1).
  \item A population is all the individuals of interest; a sample is a subset of it
        (DAT-2.A.1--2); a census measures them all (DAT-2.C.6). A sample generalizes only to
        the population it was randomly selected from (DAT-2.B.1--2).
  \item An observational study imposes no treatment --- a sample survey is one kind, and it
        may look back (retrospective) or follow forward (prospective); an experiment assigns
        treatments to experimental units (DAT-2.A.3--4). Observational data cannot establish
        cause (DAT-2.B.3).
  \item \textbf{Target misconception:} ``a study is an experiment because the data were
        collected or measured carefully.'' Precision is not a treatment. The sleep lab that
        records every minute of sleep and every cup of coffee is still \emph{watching}.
  \item \textbf{Second misconception:} ``a reasonable-sounding person picking the sample is
        as good as random'' --- the coach and his varsity players (problem~2).
\end{itemize}
\end{tabularx}
\end{skillbox}

\boxguard
\begin{skillbox}[{Vocabulary, Concepts, \& Theorems --- taught directly in the guided notes}]{greenbox}
\begin{minipage}[t]{0.48\linewidth}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{Chance-based method} --- a random process decides who is in the data
              (VAR-1.E.1)
        \item \textbf{Non-chance method} --- people decide: the researcher takes whoever is
              convenient, or respondents volunteer
        \item \textbf{Population} --- all the individuals of interest (DAT-2.A.1)
        \item \textbf{Sample} --- the subset of the population actually measured
              (DAT-2.A.2)
        \item \textbf{Census} --- measures every individual in the population (DAT-2.C.6)
    \end{itemize}
\end{minipage}
\hfill
\begin{minipage}[t]{0.48\linewidth}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{Observational study} --- observe and measure without imposing a
              treatment; a \textbf{sample survey} is one kind (DAT-2.A.3)
        \item \textbf{Experiment} --- treatments are assigned to experimental units and the
              response is measured (DAT-2.A.4)
        \item \textbf{Generalizing} --- only to the population the sample was randomly drawn
              from (DAT-2.B.1--2)
        \item \textbf{Cause} --- only a well-designed experiment; an observational study shows
              an association (DAT-2.B.3)
        \item \textbf{Carried forward} --- explanatory and response variables; association is
              not causation (Unit 2)
    \end{itemize}
\end{minipage}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Warm-Up --- Activate Prior Knowledge (5 min)]{sky}
\begin{minipage}[t]{0.48\linewidth}
    \textbf{The items and what each seeds}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{1.} A news site's online poll: 2{,}400 responses, 85\% want a later
              start. Who answers, and does 85\% describe everyone? \textbf{Seeds} the Ask How
              row --- the room's answer becomes \emph{non-chance method}.
        \item \textbf{2.} 900 students and one lunch question: a reason to ask all of them,
              and a reason to ask only some. \textbf{Seeds} the Sample row --- the word for
              ``all of them'' is \emph{census}.
        \item \textbf{3.} Sleep and test scores: explanatory and response, and whether an
              observed association shows cause. \textbf{Spirals} to Unit 2 and seeds the
              Study Type row.
    \end{itemize}
\end{minipage}
\hfill
\begin{minipage}[t]{0.48\linewidth}
    \textbf{Running it}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{Debrief item~1 aloud} and write the room's answer to ``who responds?'' on
              the board in their words (``people who care,'' ``people who want it''). Row~1
              names that a \emph{non-chance method} two minutes later.
        \item On item~2, collect one reason each way and leave them up: \emph{everyone counted}
              vs.\ \emph{time and cost}. Row~2 names the trade-off.
        \item On item~3, take the ``no, it doesn't show cause'' and ask \emph{what would?}
              Leave the question on the board; Row~3 answers it --- impose a treatment.
    \end{itemize}
\end{minipage}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Guided Notes \& Practice --- I do, then we do (34 min)]{sky}
\textbf{This is the whole lesson.} There is no group activity, and there is no independent
practice set on this page: \textbf{the homework is the individual practice}, started in class.
The notes are one \emph{Main Ideas / Notes} table --- four rows, 14 short problems.
\textbf{In every row you fill the definition lines and work the first problem of each grid; the
class works the rest, pen in their hand.} The vocabulary box (population, sample,
observational study, experiment) is filled \emph{as you go}.
\begin{multicols}{2}
\textbf{Ask How (5 min).} Fill \emph{random}, \emph{people}, \emph{untrustworthy}. Work
\textbf{problem~1} yourself (the callers chose themselves). Problems 2 and 3 are theirs.
\textbf{Problem~2 is the trap}: the coach sounds fair, but a person still chose --- and chose
the players most likely to want sports money. Keep this row moving; Lesson 3.3 names the bias.

\textbf{Sample (7 min).} Fill \emph{all}, \emph{subset}, \emph{every}, \emph{population} and
name the two vocabulary terms. Work \textbf{problem~4} (population: the school's 1{,}200
students; sample: the 100). Problem~5 is the census trade-off from the warm-up board.
Problem~6 is DAT-2.B.2 in one line: her school, not the state.

\textbf{Study Type (12 min) --- the crux.} Fill \emph{impose}, \emph{assign},
\emph{experiment}, \emph{association} and finish the vocabulary box. Work \textbf{problem~7}
(records only --- observational; note it looks back). 8 and 9 are theirs; 9 closes the loop on
warm-up item~3. \textbf{Problem~10 is the crux}: take a hand count on ``agree'' \emph{before}
anyone explains. The sleep lab measured everything and assigned nothing. Make the room say the
test back: \emph{did anyone impose a treatment?}

\columnbreak
\textbf{We do (about 10 min): The Study App.} A fresh context, problems 11--14, worked
entirely together. Students hold the pen; you hold the questions: \emph{``Who is the
population?''} \quad \emph{``Who chose the 250?''} \quad \emph{``Who decided who got the
app?''} \textbf{Problem~13 is the crux transferred} --- hours logged to the minute, still
observational --- and the one to protect when time is short; problem~14 is its counterweight,
the study that \emph{did} impose a treatment.

\textbf{The pen must actually change hands.} Put problem~11 on them cold and take a hand count
on 13 before anyone speaks. Circulate and demand the reason:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.1em, nosep]
  \item \textbf{Problem~12:} ``Could the 250 have been anyone on the list?''
  \item \textbf{Problem~13:} ``Precise --- fine. Who \emph{assigned} anything?''
  \item \textbf{Problem~14:} ``What did Study B do that Study A never did?''
\end{itemize}
While circulating, pick the papers to put up in the debrief --- one \emph{almost}-right
problem~10 (``disagree, because 50 people is too few'') and one that names the missing
treatment. \textbf{Your formative read comes twice}: here, and again in the last eight minutes
as students start the homework.
\end{multicols}
\end{skillbox}

\boxguard
\begin{skillbox}[Debrief --- whole class (8 min)]{sky}
Pens down, papers up. \textbf{This debrief is spoken} --- nothing on the page to fill in.
\begin{multicols}{2}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.3em, nosep, itemsep=3pt]
  \item \textbf{Open on the almost-right problem~10}: ``disagree, because 50 people is too
        few.'' Right verdict, wrong reason --- size has nothing to do with study type. Correct
        it gently and in public: the reason is that \emph{no treatment was imposed}.
  \item Put the three questions on the board, one per row: \emph{Who decided who is in the
        data? Whom does the sample speak for? Was a treatment imposed?} That board is the
        lesson.
  \item Take problem~2 back: the coach is a reasonable person --- why is his pick still not
        chance?
\end{enumerate}
\columnbreak
\textbf{The sentence to leave on every desk:} \emph{An experiment is something the researchers
do to the subjects, not how carefully they measure. If no one imposed a treatment, it is an
observational study, and it cannot show cause.} Make a student say it, not you.

\textbf{If the debrief runs short}, cut item~3 --- item~1 is the only one that cannot be cut.
\textbf{Do not let the debrief eat the last eight minutes} --- the homework start is your
second formative read.
\end{multicols}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[AP Practice --- extra credit, scored 1 to 5]{goldbox}
Two multiple-choice items --- \textbf{MC~1} picks the experiment for a reading program (C: 60
second graders randomly assigned; the distractors are observational designs, including a
year of careful score records and schools that chose the program), and \textbf{MC~2} picks the
appropriate generalization from 500 randomly selected Ohio adults (B: Ohio adults, with
sampling variability; (A) over-reaches to the U.S., (D) under-reaches to the 500) --- then one
free-response set on energy drinks and sleep from 1{,}500 survey records: \textbf{(a)}
observational or experiment (observational --- nothing assigned), \textbf{(b)} does it support
``energy drinks cause sleepless nights'' (no --- association only; night-shift work or stress
could drive both), and \textbf{(c)}, \textbf{the spiral}, name the display for two
quantitative variables and the pattern to expect (a scatterplot, negative association;
Unit~2).

\textbf{Extra credit, scored 1 to 5, turned in with the homework.} Go over it at the start of
Lesson 3.2. Hold the AP standard out loud: \emph{in context or it does not count} --- a part (b)
that says only ``correlation is not causation'' without naming the design or a plausible
other variable has not earned full credit.
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Homework --- scored 1 to 5, due the first class after two study halls]{goldbox}
Five items in a third context, a city's parks. \textbf{Part~A} (Riverside's dog-park survey: a
social-media poll against 400 households picked at random from 40{,}000): \textbf{A1} which
plan used chance and who decides in the other; \textbf{A2} the population and the sample;
\textbf{A3} what asking all 40{,}000 is called and why the city might not. \textbf{Part~B}:
\textbf{B1} an AP-style multiple choice (which park-lighting plan is an experiment; (C), the
rangers' careful log, is the crux distractor) with a required justification; \textbf{B2} the
crux restated --- 600 step counters, every step recorded, and a newspaper calling it an
experiment.

\textbf{Start it in the last eight minutes of the period, in class and alone.}
\textbf{Scoring:} 10 points --- A1, A2, A3, B2 at 2 each; B1 1 for the letter and 1 for the
justification. \textbf{B2 is the one to read closely}: a student who agrees with the paper has
kept the target misconception, and Lesson 3.4's experimental design assumes its absence. The
spiralbox previews Lesson 3.2.

\textbf{Paper homework is the default for this course} --- assign this page unless you have
decided otherwise. \textbf{DeltaMath override (occasional):} on the days you assign DeltaMath
instead, it replaces this page, and this page stays in the packet as extra practice. Never
assign both.
\end{skillbox}

\boxguard
\begin{skillbox}[Watch For (while circulating)]{redbox}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em, nosep]
  \item \textbf{``Measured carefully, so it is an experiment''} (notes~10 and 13, HW~B1(C) and
        B2 --- the target misconception). Probe: \emph{``What did the researchers \emph{do} to
        anyone? If the answer is `nothing but watch,' it is observational.''}
  \item \textbf{Calling any reasonable-sounding pick ``random''} (notes~2). Probe: \emph{``Could
        the result change depending on who did the picking? Then it was not chance.''}
  \item \textbf{Generalizing past the population} (notes~6, AP~MC2). Probe: \emph{``Who was on
        the list the sample came from? That is who it speaks for.''}
  \item \textbf{Naming the sample as a number} (``Sample: 100'') or the population as the
        sample (notes~4 and 11, HW~A2). Probe: \emph{``100 \emph{what}? Name the
        individuals.''}
\end{itemize}
Cold-call prompts: \emph{``Name a study that measures very carefully but is not an
experiment.''} \quad \emph{``What would you have to change to make the sleep lab an
experiment?''}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Close \& Assign (8 min)]{goldbox}
\textbf{Students start the homework here, in class, alone and silent --- this is the individual
practice, and the first minutes of it are your best formative read.} Hand the launch first ---
five items on paper, scored, due the first class after two study halls --- and point out that
the AP Practice page is extra credit, turned in with the homework. Circulate on \textbf{Part~B
item~2}. Sort what you see into three piles as you walk --- said observational because no one
was assigned a dog (ready); said ``not an experiment'' with no reason, or ``because 600 is too
few'' (ready, needs the language); agreed with the paper (the misconception survived) --- and
open Lesson 3.2 by returning to the sleep lab if that third pile ran past a third of the room.
Then close on what changed: \emph{``You walked in asking whether a result is true. You walk out
asking how it was collected.''} \textbf{Preview:} Lesson 3.2, \emph{Random Sampling Methods}
--- how to let chance pick the sample: simple random, stratified, and cluster samples.
\end{skillbox}

\begin{teachernote}[Warm-Up]
Five minutes total; each item seeds one notes row, so do not resolve any of them --- leave
them on the board. On item~1, write the room's answer to ``who answers?'' in their words. On
item~2, one reason each way is enough. Item~3 is a Unit~2 spiral; take 90 seconds, get ``no,
it doesn't show cause,'' and ask \emph{what would?} Row~3 answers it.
\end{teachernote}

\begin{teachernote}[Guided Notes \& Practice]
\textbf{Pace it 24 / 10, then 8 for the debrief, then 8 for the homework start.} Four rows, 14
problems. \textbf{You work 1, 4, and 7}; the class works the rest. Ask How (5 min) and Sample (7
min) must move, because the time belongs to Study Type (12 min), which carries the crux.
\textbf{Problem~2 is the trap} (a reasonable person is still a person); \textbf{problem~10 is the
crux} (a carefully measured sleep lab is still observational). Take a hand count on 10 before
any explanation. \textbf{The Study App is where the pen changes hands}; problem~13 is the crux
transferred and the one to protect. If you only get through 11, 13, and 14, that is the right
trade. \textbf{Your second formative read is the homework start}: Part~B item~2, three piles.
\textbf{Debrief (8 min), spoken}, opening on the almost-right problem~10. \textbf{Do not let the
debrief eat the last eight minutes} --- the homework start is not optional padding.
\end{teachernote}

\begin{teachernote}[AP Practice]
\textbf{This page is extra credit, scored 1 to 5} --- the graded practice is the homework
behind it. \textbf{MC~1 is the one worth reading closely}: (D), a year of careful records, is
the target misconception in AP clothing; if more than a handful choose it or (A), return to
the sleep lab before Lesson 3.4. On MC~2, (D) is the over-cautious answer --- a random sample
\emph{does} speak for the population it came from. On the free response, part (b) needs the
design (nothing imposed) \emph{and} the context; part (c) is a Unit~2 spiral.
\end{teachernote}

``dog owners.'' On B1, (D) with no justification earns 1 of 2. \textbf{Paper is the default.}
On the occasions you assign DeltaMath in its place, tell students this page is now optional
practice. Do not assign both.
\end{teachernote}
