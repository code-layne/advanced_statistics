\small
For each scenario, check each BINS condition. Write a brief reason.
Circle \textbf{Binomial} or \textbf{Not Binomial}.

\vspace{8pt}
\textbf{Scenario 1:} You flip a fair coin until you get 3 heads.
Let $X =$ the number of flips needed.

\vspace{4pt}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{} p{0.08\linewidth} X p{2.8cm} @{}}
B: & \ans{Binary: heads or tails on each flip.} & \ans{Met} \;/\; Not Met \\[6pt]
I: & \ans{Independent: flips do not affect each other.} & \ans{Met} \;/\; Not Met \\[6pt]
N: & \ans{No fixed $n$ --- flip until 3 heads.} & Met \;/\; \ans{Not Met} \\[6pt]
S: & \ans{Same: $p = 0.5$ on every flip.} & \ans{Met} \;/\; Not Met \\[6pt]
\end{tabularx}
\vspace{4pt}
Verdict: \quad \textbf{Binomial} \qquad / \qquad \ans{Not Binomial}

\vspace{14pt}
\textbf{Scenario 2:} A bag holds 3 red and 7 blue chips. You draw 5 chips
\emph{with replacement}. Let $X =$ the number of red chips.

\vspace{4pt}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{} p{0.08\linewidth} X p{2.8cm} @{}}
B: & \ans{Binary: red or blue on each draw.} & \ans{Met} \;/\; Not Met \\[6pt]
I: & \ans{Independent: replacement resets the bag.} & \ans{Met} \;/\; Not Met \\[6pt]
N: & \ans{Fixed: $n = 5$ draws.} & \ans{Met} \;/\; Not Met \\[6pt]
S: & \ans{Same: $p = 3/10 = 0.3$ every draw.} & \ans{Met} \;/\; Not Met \\[6pt]
\end{tabularx}
\vspace{4pt}
Verdict: \quad \ans{Binomial} \qquad / \qquad \textbf{Not Binomial}
\vspace{4pt}
\end{tcolorbox}

% ── TIER A ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=sky, colframe=navy, boxrule=1pt, arc=2mm,
    title={\bfseries Tier A \quad Approaching Proficiency: Computing Binomial Probabilities},
    fonttitle=\small]
\small

\textbf{Scenario 3:} A factory produces smartphones. Historically, 5\% of phones
are defective. A quality inspector selects 12 phones at random from a large shipment
(population size is in the thousands).

\vspace{6pt}
(a) Check BINS. For each condition, write a sentence from this context.

\vspace{4pt}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{} p{0.08\linewidth} X @{}}
B: & \ansline{Each phone is defective (success) or not (failure).} \\[4pt]
I: & \ansline{$12 < 10\%$ of thousands, so treat picks as independent.} \\[4pt]
N: & \ansline{Fixed: $n = 12$ phones chosen in advance.} \\[4pt]
S: & \ansline{Same: $p = 0.05$ for every phone.} \\[4pt]
\end{tabularx}

\vspace{4pt}
State the distribution: $X \sim$ \ans{$B(12,\;0.05)$}

\vspace{10pt}
(b) Find $P(X = 1)$ --- the probability that exactly 1 phone is defective.
Show the formula setup.

\vspace{4pt}
\[
P(X=1) = \binom{{\color{keyred}\mathbf{12}}}{{\color{keyred}\mathbf{1}}} ({\color{keyred}\mathbf{0.05}})^1 ({\color{keyred}\mathbf{0.95}})^{11} = {\color{keyred}\mathbf{12(0.05)(0.5688) \approx 0.3413}} \hspace{4cm}
\]

\vspace{14pt}
(c) Find $P(X = 0)$ --- no defective phones.

\vspace{4pt}
\[
P(X=0) = {\color{keyred}\mathbf{(0.95)^{12} \approx 0.5404}} \hspace{5cm}
\]

\vspace{6pt}
Calculator check: \texttt{binompdf(}\ans{12}\texttt{,}\;\ans{0.05}\texttt{,}\;\texttt{0)} $= $ \ans{0.5404}
\vspace{4pt}
\end{tcolorbox}

    fonttitle=\small]
\small

\textbf{Scenario 4:} A small school has 80 students. A class of 10 is selected
at random without replacement. Suppose 20\% of all students own a graphing calculator.
Let $X =$ the number of students in the class who own a graphing calculator.

\vspace{6pt}
(a) Is the $10\%$ condition satisfied? Show the calculation and interpret.

\vspace{4pt}
\ansline{$0.10 \times 80 = 8$ and $n = 10 > 8$, so the condition is \textbf{not} met.}\\[1pt]\ansline{Draws are not quite independent; the binomial is only an approximation.}

\vspace{8pt}
(b) Assuming the $10\%$ condition allows you to proceed, state the approximate
distribution of $X$ and compute $P(X = 3)$.

\vspace{4pt}
$X \approx B({\color{keyred}\mathbf{10}},\;{\color{keyred}\mathbf{0.20}})$ \hspace{1cm}
$P(X = 3) = $ \ans{$\binom{10}{3}(0.20)^3(0.80)^7$} $\approx$ \ans{0.2013}

\vspace{10pt}
(c) Build the partial distribution table for $X = 0, 1, 2, 3$ and verify the
probabilities are increasing then decreasing (the typical bell-shape pattern for
a binomial with $p = 0.2$, $n = 10$).

\vspace{6pt}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.8}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{} >{\bfseries}p{1.2cm} X X X X @{}}
$k$ & 0 & 1 & 2 & 3 \\
\hline
$P(X=k)$ & \ans{0.1074} & \ans{0.2684} & \ans{0.3020} & \ans{0.2013} \\
\end{tabularx}
\vspace{4pt}
\end{tcolorbox}
