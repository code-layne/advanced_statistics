% ── ROW 1: center — mean and median ──────────────────────────────────────────
\mainidea[Two measures of]{Center} &
A \textbf{statistic} is a number computed from sample data. The \textbf{mean}
$\bar x = \sum x_i / n$ is the sum over the count. The \textbf{median} is the middle of the
\emph{ordered} data (even count: average the two middle values).

\vspace{2pt}

% ── ROW 2: position — quartiles and percentiles ──────────────────────────────
\mainidea[Position]{Quartiles} &
The \textbf{quartiles} cut the ordered data into quarters: $Q_1$ is the median of the lower
half, $Q_3$ the median of the upper half, and the middle 50\% sits between them. The $p$th
\textbf{percentile} is the value with $p\%$ of the data at or below it, so $Q_1$ is the 25th
percentile and $Q_3$ the 75th.

% ── ROW 3: spread — range, IQR, standard deviation ───────────────────────────
\mainidea[Three measures of]{Spread} &
Each measure of spread is \textbf{one number, not an interval}. The \textbf{range} is
maximum $-$ minimum. The \textbf{IQR} is $Q_3 - Q_1$, the width of the middle half. The
\textbf{standard deviation} $s_x = \sqrt{\dfrac{1}{n-1}\sum (x_i - \bar x)^2}$ is roughly the
typical distance of a value from the mean. Technology computes it; we do it by hand once, on
a five-package route: 26, 28, 30, 32, 34 minutes, so $\bar x = 30$.
\\
& \begin{center}
\begin{tikzpicture}[scale=0.9]
  \draw[->, thick] (-0.3,0) -- (10.3,0);
  \foreach \x/\lab in {0/24, 1.6/26, 3.2/28, 4.8/30, 6.4/32, 8.0/34, 9.6/36} {
    \draw (\x,-0.08) -- (\x,0.08); \node[below, font=\tiny] at (\x,-0.1) {\lab};}
  \foreach \x in {1.6, 3.2, 4.8, 6.4, 8.0} { \fill[navy] (\x,0.3) circle (0.11); }
  \node[font=\tiny] at (4.8,-0.6) {Minutes to deliver};
  \draw[goldacc, very thick, dashed] (4.8,-0.15) -- (4.8,1.9);
  \node[font=\scriptsize\bfseries, above] at (4.8,1.9) {$\bar x = 30$};
  \draw[<-, navy, thick] (1.6,1.35) -- (4.8,1.35);
  \node[font=\scriptsize, above] at (3.2,1.35) {$26 - 30 = -4$};
  \draw[<-, navy, thick] (3.2,0.7) -- (4.8,0.7);
  \node[font=\scriptsize, above] at (4.0,0.7) {$-2$};

  \node[font=\scriptsize, above] at (5.6,0.7) {$+2$};
  \draw[->, navy, thick] (4.8,1.35) -- (8.0,1.35);
  \node[font=\scriptsize, above] at (6.4,1.35) {$34 - 30 = +4$};
  \node[font=\tiny, anchor=west] at (8.3,0.5) {deviations $x_i - \bar x$};
\end{tikzpicture}

Square the deviations, add, divide by $n-1$ (that is the \textbf{variance} $s_x^2$), take the root:
\begin{work}
s_x^2 &= \tfrac{16+4+0+4+16}{5-1} = \tfrac{40}{4} = 10, \qquad s_x = \sqrt{10} \approx 3.16 \text{ min}
\end{work}

% ── ROW 4: resistance — THE CRUX ─────────────────────────────────────────────
\mainidea[One value changes:]{Resistance} &
A statistic is \textbf{resistant} if one extreme value barely moves it (the median, the IQR)
and \textbf{nonresistant} if one extreme value drags it along (the mean, the range, $s_x$).
Both centers are correct; the \textbf{shape} picks the pair to report: skewed or an outlier
present, \textbf{median and IQR}; roughly symmetric, \textbf{mean and $s_x$}. An
\textbf{outlier} is more than $1.5\times\text{IQR}$ beyond a quartile.
