\begin{vocabbox}
\small
Fill in each term \textbf{as we name it} in the notes below.
\par
\termans{Statistic}{a numerical summary of sample data --- the mean, the median, and every other number computed today.}
\par
\termans{Mean $\bar x$ and median}{mean: the sum of the values over the count; median: the middle of the ordered data (with an even count, average the two middle values).}
\par
\termans{Quartiles $Q_1$, $Q_3$ and percentile}{$Q_1$ and $Q_3$ are the medians of the lower and upper halves; the $p$th percentile is the value with $p\%$ of the data at or below it.}
\par
\termans{Range and interquartile range (IQR)}{range $=$ maximum $-$ minimum; IQR $= Q_3 - Q_1$. Each is one number, not an interval.}
\par
\termans{Standard deviation $s_x$}{roughly the typical distance of a value from the mean; computed by technology. Its square $s_x^2$ is the variance.}
\par
\termans{Resistant / nonresistant}{resistant --- an outlier barely moves it (median, IQR); nonresistant --- an outlier drags it (mean, standard deviation, range).}
\par
\end{vocabbox}

\begin{guidednotes}
% ── ROW 1: center — mean and median ──────────────────────────────────────────
\mainidea[Two measures of]{Center} &
A \textbf{statistic} is a numerical summary of \ans{sample} data. \textbf{Nine Paychecks:} a
landscaping crew of eight and their boss took home last week, in dollars:

\vspace{2pt}
{\centering
800 \quad 3000 \quad 650 \quad 750 \quad 700 \quad 850 \quad 700 \quad 800 \quad 750
\par}
\\
& The \textbf{mean} $\bar x$ adds every value and divides by \ans{the number of values}:
$\bar x = \sum x_i / n$. The nine paychecks add to \$9{,}000, so
\begin{work}
\bar x &= \tfrac{9000}{9} = \text{\$1{,}000}
\end{work}
The \textbf{median} is the \ans{middle} value of the \emph{ordered} data --- with an even
count, the \ans{average} of the two middle values. Order the nine paychecks first:

\vspace{2pt}
{\centering
\ans{650} \; \ans{700} \; \ans{700} \; \ans{750} \; \ans{750} \;
\ans{800} \; \ans{800} \; \ans{850} \; \ans{3000}
\par}
\vspace{2pt}
The fifth of nine is the median: \ans{\$750}. Both numbers are \textbf{correct}.
\\
& \notesprompt{Use the paychecks.}
\begin{probgrid}{|Y|Y|Y|}
\pcell{1}{Who takes home the mean, \$1{,}000?}{1.1cm}{Nobody: eight earn less, one far more} &
\pcell{2}{Who earns the median?}{1.1cm}{The 5th in order: a crew member, \$750} &
\pcell{3}{The owner says ``we average \$1{,}000.'' Which measure? Is she wrong?}{1.1cm}{The mean --- and both are correct} \\ \hline
\end{probgrid}
\\ \hline
% ── ROW 2: position — quartiles and percentiles ──────────────────────────────
\mainidea[Position]{Quartiles} &
Twelve delivery times on a courier company's Route A, in minutes, already in order:

\vspace{2pt}
{\centering
27 \quad 28 \quad 28 \quad 29 \quad 29 \quad 30 \quad 30 \quad 31 \quad 31 \quad 32 \quad
32 \quad 33
\par}
\vspace{2pt}
Twelve values, so the median is halfway between the 6th and the 7th: $(30+30)/2 =$
\ans{30} minutes. Now \textbf{split each half at its own middle}: the \textbf{first
quartile} $Q_1$ is the median of the \ans{lower} half, the \textbf{third quartile} $Q_3$
the median of the \ans{upper} half, and between them sits the middle \ans{50}\%
of the data. The $p$th \textbf{percentile} is the value with $p\%$ of the data
\ans{at or below} it: $Q_1$ is the \ans{25}th percentile, $Q_3$ the \ans{75}th.
\textbf{In context:} a 31.5-minute package sits at the 75th percentile --- about 75\% of Route A's
packages took \ans{31.5} minutes or less.
\\
& \notesprompt{Use Route A.}
\begin{probgrid}{|Y|Y|Y|}
\pcell{4}{$Q_1$ --- the middle of 27 28 28 29 29 30}{0.9cm}{28.5 minutes} &
\pcell{5}{$Q_3$ --- the middle of 30 31 31 32 32 33}{0.9cm}{31.5 minutes} &
\pcell{6}{How many of the twelve took $Q_1$ minutes or less?}{0.9cm}{3 of 12: a quarter} \\ \hline
\end{probgrid}
\\ \hline
% ── ROW 3: spread — range, IQR, standard deviation ───────────────────────────
\mainidea[Three measures of]{Spread} &
Each one is a \textbf{single number, not an interval}. The \textbf{range} is \ans{maximum}
$-$ \ans{minimum}. The \textbf{interquartile range (IQR)} is \ans{$Q_3$} $-$
\ans{$Q_1$}, the width of the middle half. For Route A:
\begin{work}
\text{range} &= 33 - 27 = 6 \text{ min}, \qquad \text{IQR} = 31.5 - 28.5 = 3 \text{ min}
\end{work}
\\
& The \textbf{standard deviation} $s_x$ measures spread around the \emph{mean}: roughly the
\ans{typical} distance of a value from $\bar x$. Once, by hand, on a tiny fourth route:
five packages at 26, 28, 30, 32, 34 minutes, so $\bar x = 30$.
\par\vspace{2pt}
\textbf{Deviations} $x_i - \bar x$: \; $-4$, \; \ans{$-2$}, \; 0, \; \ans{2}, \; 4.
Square them, add, divide by $n-1$ (the \textbf{variance} $s_x^2$), take the root:
$s_x = \sqrt{\frac{1}{n-1}\sum (x_i-\bar x)^2}$.
\begin{work}
s_x^2 &= \tfrac{16+4+0+4+16}{5-1} = \tfrac{40}{4} = 10, \qquad s_x = \sqrt{10} \approx 3.16 \text{ min}
\end{work}
\textbf{Technology computes $s_x$ from here on.} It is built from the \ans{mean} and carries
the \ans{units} of the variable.
\\
& \notesprompt{Use the five-package route.}
\begin{probgrid}{|Y|Y|Y|}
\pcell{7}{Its range and its IQR (halves 26, 28 and 32, 34)}{1.2cm}{Range $34-26=8$; IQR $33-27=6$ min} &
\pcell{8}{A student writes Route A's range as ``27 to 33.'' What is wrong?}{1.2cm}{One number: $33-27=6$ min} &
\pcell{9}{In seconds instead: what happens to $\bar x$, the range, and $s_x$?}{1.2cm}{All $\times 60$: 1800, 480, about 190 s} \\ \hline
\end{probgrid}
\\ \hline
% ── ROW 4: resistance — THE CRUX ─────────────────────────────────────────────
\mainidea[One value changes:]{Resistance} &
Back to the paychecks: \textbf{the owner's \$3{,}000 becomes \$750.} Recompute both centers:
\begin{work}
\bar x_{\text{new}} &= \tfrac{6750}{9} = \text{\$750}, \qquad \text{median}_{\text{new}} = \text{5th value} = \text{\$750}
\end{work}
The mean fell by \ans{\$250}; the median moved \ans{not at all} --- it asks only
which value is \ans{fifth} in order, and that position never saw the \$3{,}000.
\\
& \textbf{Route B's} twelve times also add to 360 minutes ($Q_1 = 23$, $Q_3 = 37$). \textbf{Route C is
Route A with one late package:} its 33-minute delivery became 51.

& \textbf{Resistant} --- one extreme value barely moves it: the \ans{median} and the
\ans{IQR}. \textbf{Nonresistant} --- one extreme value drags it: the \ans{mean},
the \ans{standard deviation}, and the \ans{range}.
\textbf{Which pair to report?} Both centers are correct; the \textbf{shape} decides. Skewed, or an
outlier present: \ans{median} and \ans{IQR}. Roughly symmetric, no outliers:
\ans{mean} and \ans{standard deviation}. \textbf{Outlier rules:} more than
$1.5\times\text{IQR}$ beyond a quartile, or 2 or more standard deviations from the mean.
\\
