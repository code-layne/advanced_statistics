    \textbf{Primary Objective:} Students will explain that a number carries meaning only
    when placed in context, identify the individuals and variables in a data set, and
    distinguish a statistical question --- one that anticipates variability and is answered
    by collecting data --- from a question with a single definite answer (Big Idea: VAR).
\end{tcolorbox}

\boxguard
\begin{skillbox}[Priority Ideas \& Skills]{goldbox}
\begin{minipage}[t]{0.48\linewidth}
    \textbf{AP Skill 1 --- Selecting Statistical Methods}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Identify the question to be answered or the problem to be solved
              (Skill 1.A).
        \item Identify questions that can be answered from variation in one-variable
              data (VAR-1.A).
        \item Attach context to a bare number: who was measured, what was measured,
              and in what units (VAR-1.A.1).
    \end{itemize}
\end{minipage}
\hfill
\begin{minipage}[t]{0.48\linewidth}
    \textbf{Key Understandings}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Numbers convey meaningful information only when placed in context
              (EK VAR-1.A.1).
        \item Data are measurements taken on \emph{individuals}; a \emph{variable} is a
              characteristic that changes from one individual to the next.
        \item Variability is the reason statistics exists --- if every individual gave
              the same value, one measurement would answer everything.
        \item A statistical question \emph{anticipates} variability in its answers;
              a non-statistical question has one definite answer.
    \end{itemize}
\end{minipage}
\end{skillbox}

\boxguard
\begin{skillbox}[{Vocabulary, Concepts, \& Theorems}]{greenbox}
\begin{minipage}[t]{0.48\linewidth}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{Statistics} --- the science of collecting, organizing, analyzing,
              and drawing conclusions from data
        \item \textbf{Data} --- recorded values, together with the context that gives
              them meaning
        \item \textbf{Context} --- who/what was measured, when, where, why, and in what
              units (EK VAR-1.A.1)
        \item \textbf{Individual} --- the person, animal, or object described by a row
              of the data set
    \end{itemize}
\end{minipage}
\hfill
\begin{minipage}[t]{0.48\linewidth}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{Variable} --- a characteristic recorded for each individual; it
              takes different values for different individuals
        \item \textbf{Variability} --- the fact that values differ across individuals;
              the reason a single measurement is not enough
        \item \textbf{Statistical question} --- a question that anticipates variability
              and is answered by collecting and analyzing data
        \item \textbf{Data analysis process} --- ask a question $\to$ collect data $\to$
              analyze $\to$ interpret in context
    \end{itemize}
\end{minipage}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Activate Prior Knowledge \& Spiral Review]{sky}
\begin{minipage}[t]{0.48\linewidth}
    \textbf{Prior Knowledge Check (3--4 min)}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textit{``In math so far, how many answers has a question usually had?''}
              (one) --- today we meet questions whose answers vary.
        \item \textit{``What does the word `average' mean to you?''} Collect informal
              language; do not correct it yet.
        \item Transition: \textit{``If I tell you the number is 68, do you know anything?
              What would you need to know?''}
    \end{itemize}
    \textit{(This is the course's first lesson --- there is no prior unit to spiral.
    The warm-up serves as the entry point rather than a review.)}
\end{minipage}
\hfill
\begin{minipage}[t]{0.48\linewidth}
    \textbf{Connections Forward}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Lesson 1.1: the language of variation --- classifying variables as
              categorical or quantitative
        \item Lessons 1.2--1.5: representing and describing the variability we name today
        \item Unit 3: where the data come from --- sampling and study design formalize
              \emph{population} and \emph{sample}
        \item Units 6--9: inference quantifies how much of the variability is random
              (EU VAR-1: conclusions are uncertain)

\boxguard
\begin{skillbox}[Hook \& Lesson]{sky}
\begin{minipage}[t]{0.48\linewidth}
    \textbf{Hook (5 min)}\\
    Write a single number on the board: \textbf{68}. Say nothing else. Ask:
    \textit{``What does this tell you?''}\\[4pt]
    Let students guess --- a test score, a temperature, a heart rate, a height in inches.
    Reveal that all of these are plausible, which is the point: \textbf{a number with no
    context carries no information}. Then add the context one piece at a time (``inches''
    $\to$ ``height'' $\to$ ``a student in this class'') and let them watch the number
    become meaningful.
\end{minipage}
\hfill
\begin{minipage}[t]{0.48\linewidth}
    \textbf{Lesson Flow (18--20 min)}
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item Define \emph{statistics}, \emph{data}, and \emph{context}; name the
              W questions (who, what, when, where, why, how).
        \item Show a small data table (8 shelter dogs). Identify the \emph{individuals}
              (rows) and the \emph{variables} (columns).
        \item Ask ``how tall is Maria?'' vs.\ ``how tall are students in this class?'' ---
              the first has one answer, the second has many. Name the difference:
              \textbf{variability}.
        \item Build the test for a statistical question: \emph{does it anticipate
              variability, and is it answered by collecting data?}
        \item Close with the data analysis process as the arc of the whole course:
              ask $\to$ collect $\to$ analyze $\to$ interpret.
    \end{enumerate}
\end{minipage}
\end{skillbox}

\boxguard
\begin{skillbox}[Explicit Instruction \& Active Monitoring]{sky}
\begin{minipage}[t]{0.48\linewidth}
    \textbf{Direct Instruction (10 min)}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Emphasize EK VAR-1.A.1 in the students' own words: \textit{numbers mean
              nothing until you say what they measure and who they came from.}
        \item Individuals are \emph{rows}, variables are \emph{columns} --- draw the
              arrows on the projected table so the layout becomes automatic.
        \item A variable must actually \emph{vary}. If every row shows the same value,
              it is a constant of the data set, not a variable worth studying.
        \item The statistical-question test, stated as two checks: (1)~will the answers
              differ across individuals? (2)~would I have to collect data to answer it?
              Both yes $\to$ statistical.
    \end{itemize}
\end{minipage}
\hfill
\begin{minipage}[t]{0.48\linewidth}
    \textbf{Active Monitoring}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Common error: naming a \emph{value} (``golden retriever'') when asked for
              the \emph{variable} (``breed''). Probe: \textit{``Is that the category, or
              the heading of the column?''}
        \item Common error: calling any question with a number in it statistical.
              Probe: \textit{``How many answers does that question have? If it's one,
              it isn't statistical.''}
        \item Common error: treating ``How many students are in this class?'' as
              statistical --- it is a single count, not a distribution.
        \item Cold-call: \textit{``Give me the individual and one variable from your row
              of the table.''} Rotate so every group answers once.
    \end{itemize}
\end{minipage}
\end{skillbox}

\boxguard
\begin{skillbox}[Group Work \& Differentiation]{redbox}
\begin{minipage}[t]{0.48\linewidth}
    \textbf{Group Activity (10--12 min)}\\
    Three differentiated tiers (activity sheet):
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textit{Tier R}: Given a completed data table, name the individuals and
              variables, then choose the statistical question from three options and
              say why the other two fail.
        \item \textit{Tier A}: Given a context with no table, write two statistical
              questions and explain what varies in each.
        \item \textit{Tier E}: Given a headline that reports a bare number, list the
              context the headline omits and write the statistical question the study
              behind it must have asked.
    \end{itemize}
\end{minipage}
\hfill
\begin{minipage}[t]{0.48\linewidth}
    \textbf{Differentiation}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textit{Support}: Provide the two-check test on a sentence strip taped to
              the desk; students apply it aloud before writing.
        \item \textit{Extension}: Ask Tier E groups what data they would have to collect
              to check the headline's claim --- a first look at study design (Unit 3).
        \item \textit{Technology}: Project a live class data set (heights, reaction
              times) collected in the first two minutes; use it as the running example.
        \item \textit{ELL}: Pair the vocabulary cards (individual / variable / value)
              with the projected table so each word points at a physical part of it.
    \end{itemize}
\end{minipage}
\end{skillbox}

\boxguard
\begin{skillbox}[Individual Work \& Assessment]{redbox}
\begin{minipage}[t]{0.48\linewidth}
    \textbf{Exit Ticket (5 min)}
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item Choose the statistical question from four options.
        \item Given the bare claim ``the average is 12,'' name two pieces of missing
              context.
        \item Given a short scenario, identify the individual and one variable, then
              write a statistical question about it.
    \end{enumerate}
    Items 1 and 3 are the readiness check for Lesson 1.1.
\end{minipage}
\hfill
\begin{minipage}[t]{0.48\linewidth}
    \textbf{AP-Style Check}\\
    \textit{``A veterinarian records the weight, breed, and age of each of 40 dogs seen
    in one week. Which of the following is a statistical question about this data set?''}
    \begin{enumerate}[label=(\Alph*)]
        \item How many dogs did the veterinarian see this week?
        \item What is the breed of the third dog on the list?
        \item How do the weights of the 40 dogs vary?
        \item Is the veterinarian's office open on Sunday?
    \end{enumerate}
    \textit{Answer: (C) --- the only option whose answer differs across individuals.}
\end{minipage}
\end{skillbox}

\boxguard
\begin{skillbox}[Reinforcement \& Extension]{goldbox}
\begin{minipage}[t]{0.48\linewidth}
    \textbf{Homework / Practice}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item A shelter data set (8 dogs, 5 variables): identify individuals and
              variables, count them, and name a value versus a variable.
        \item Sort six questions as statistical or not, each with a one-line reason.
        \item Critique a claim reported with no context; supply the missing W questions.
        \item Reflect: \textit{``Why does statistics exist at all --- what would be true
              of the world if nothing varied?''}
    \end{itemize}
\end{minipage}
\hfill
\begin{minipage}[t]{0.48\linewidth}
    \textbf{Extension / Preview}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textit{Extension}: Find a number reported in a news headline. List every
              piece of context the headline leaves out, then write the statistical
              question the underlying study had to ask.
        \item \textit{Preview}: Lesson 1.1 splits variables into \emph{categorical} and
              \emph{quantitative} --- the first decision that determines every graph and
              summary we use for the rest of the course.
    \end{itemize}
\end{minipage}
\end{skillbox}

% ── Teacher notes — one per component, in packet order ────────────────────────
\begin{teachernote}[Warm-Up]
Deliberately unanswerable at first glance. Item~1 gives seven numbers and no context, and
the intended student response is \emph{``I can't tell.''} Resist rescuing them --- the
discomfort is the lesson. Give 3~minutes, then debrief by asking what they would need to
know; their list becomes the W questions you formalize in the notes. Expect some students
to guess ``test scores'' and defend it; validate the reasoning while pointing out that a
guess is not information. Item~3 is the first appearance of the statistical-question test,
so leave it on the board through the whole period.
\end{teachernote}

\begin{teachernote}[Guided Notes]
Pace the vocabulary block quickly --- the terms are intuitive and the work is in applying
them. Spend the saved time on the individuals-and-variables table: project it and physically
trace a row (one individual) and a column (one variable) with your finger. Note that
\emph{population} and \emph{sample} are deliberately \textbf{not} introduced here --- they
belong to Unit~3, and naming them today competes with the individual/variable distinction
this lesson is built on. In the guided practice, the recurring error is naming a value where
a variable belongs --- have students point at the column heading before writing.
\end{teachernote}

\begin{teachernote}[Group Activity]
Assign tiers by the warm-up, not by prior grades --- this is day one and you have no other
signal. Tier~R groups need the two-check test read aloud before they start. Circulate for the
Tier~A groups' explanation of \emph{what varies}; a question can look statistical and still
be about a single individual. Tier~E is the richest debrief: the missing context in a headline
is usually the sample, which sets up Unit~3. Reserve the last three minutes so one group from
each tier reports a single question to the class.
\end{teachernote}

\begin{teachernote}[Exit Ticket]
Item~1's distractor (B) draws students who equate ``has a number in it'' with ``statistical.''
If more than a third choose it, reteach the two-check test at the start of Lesson~1.1 rather
than moving on. Item~2 is scored on naming any two of who / what / units / when --- accept
student phrasing. Item~3 is the readiness signal: a student who writes a question with one
definite answer has not yet got variability, and should be seated near a Tier~E partner
tomorrow.
\end{teachernote}

\begin{teachernote}[Homework]
The shelter table is small on purpose so that counting variables is quick and the thinking
stays on the individual/variable distinction. Question~4 (the context critique) is the one
worth grading closely --- it is EK VAR-1.A.1 in its most transferable form and it recurs on
the AP exam as ``interpret in context.'' The reflection has no wrong answer; look for any
recognition that statistics exists \emph{because} values differ. Extension is optional and
pairs well with a bulletin-board display of headlines students bring in.
\end{teachernote}
