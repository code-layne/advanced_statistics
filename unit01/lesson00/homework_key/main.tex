\boxguard[30]
\begin{scenariobox}[The Shelter Data Set]{navy}
\small
A county animal shelter posts this table of the dogs currently available for adoption.

\vspace{8pt}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.3}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{} l Y Y Y Y @{}}
\rowcolor{sky}
\textbf{Name} & \textbf{Breed} & \textbf{Age (yr)} & \textbf{Weight (lb)} & \textbf{Days at shelter} \\
\hline
Biscuit & Beagle    & 3 & 24 & 12 \\
Momo    & Terrier   & 1 & 15 & 44 \\
Ranger  & Shepherd  & 5 & 61 &  6 \\
Pepper  & Retriever & 2 & 55 & 21 \\
Sage    & Beagle    & 7 & 27 & 59 \\
Tuck    & Mixed     & 4 & 38 &  9 \\
Willow  & Terrier   & 2 & 17 & 33 \\
Zeke    & Shepherd  & 6 & 70 & 15 \\
\hline
\end{tabularx}
\end{scenariobox}

\vspace{0.14in}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.7em, itemsep=0.18in]

  \item \textbf{Individuals and variables.}
        \vspace{4pt}

        \textbf{(a)} One individual in this data set is \ans{one dog, e.g.\ Sage}.

        \vspace{6pt}
        \textbf{(b)} There are \ans{8} individuals and \ans{5} variables.

        \vspace{6pt}
        \textbf{(c)} ``Shepherd'' is a \ans{value} of the variable \ans{Breed}.

        \vspace{6pt}
        \textbf{(d)} Name one characteristic of these dogs that is \emph{not} recorded
        here but could have been.\\[4pt]
        \ansline{Sex, color, height, or whether the dog is house-trained. (Any reasonable one.)}

  \item \textbf{Statistical or not?} Mark each \textbf{S} or \textbf{N}.

        \vspace{4pt}
        \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.75}
        \begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{} X C{2.2cm} @{}}
        \hline
        \textbf{Question} & \textbf{S or N} \\
        \hline
        How much does Zeke weigh?                              & \ans{N} \\
        How do the weights of the shelter's dogs vary?         & \ans{S} \\
        How many dogs are listed in the table?                 & \ans{N} \\
        How long do dogs typically wait to be adopted?         & \ans{S} \\
        Is Momo a terrier?                                     & \ans{N} \\
        Do heavier dogs tend to wait longer than lighter ones? & \ans{S} \\
        \hline
        \end{tabularx}

        \vspace{8pt}
        Pick one question you marked \textbf{N} and rewrite it so that it becomes
        statistical.\\[4pt]
        \ansline{``How much does Zeke weigh?'' names one dog, so it has one answer. Rewrite:}\\[1pt]\ansline{``How do the weights of the shelter's dogs vary?'' --- now the answers differ.}

  \item \textbf{A claim with no context.} A flyer on the shelter door reads:

        \vspace{6pt}
        \begin{center}
        {\large\bfseries\color{navy} ``Our dogs go home in 25 days.''}
        \end{center}

        \vspace{6pt}
        \textbf{(a)} List \textbf{three} W questions the flyer fails to answer.\\[4pt]
        \ansline{1. WHO --- which dogs, and how many of them went into this figure?}\\[1pt]\ansline{2. WHAT --- is 25 an average, a typical wait, or the longest anyone waited?}\\[1pt]\ansline{3. WHEN --- over what period was this measured? Adoption rates change.}

        \vspace{6pt}
        \textbf{(b)} Using the table above, give one specific reason a visitor should
        not expect \emph{their} dog to go home in 25 days.\\[4pt]
        \ansline{The waits in the table run from 6 days to 59 days --- they vary widely.}\\[1pt]\ansline{A single summary number describes the group, not any one dog.}

  \item \textbf{Write your own.} Write one statistical question about the shelter data
        set, and say exactly what varies in it.\\[4pt]
        \ansline{``How do the ages of the shelter's dogs vary?'' What varies is the age,}\\[1pt]\ansline{in years, from one dog to the next --- it ranges from 1 to 7 in this table.}

\end{enumerate}

\vspace{0.12in}

\boxguard[14]
\begin{reflectionbox}
\small
Statistics exists because values differ. Suppose they did not --- suppose every dog at
the shelter had the same weight, the same age, and the same wait. What would be left for
a statistician to do, and why?\\[4pt]
\ansline{Almost nothing. If every dog were identical, measuring one dog would tell you}\\[1pt]\ansline{everything about all of them --- no summary, graph, or sample would be needed.}\\[1pt]\ansline{Statistics is the study of variability, so with no variability there is no work.}
\end{reflectionbox}

\begin{extensionbox}
\small
Find a number reported in a news headline this week. Write down the headline, then list
every piece of context it leaves out. Finish by writing the statistical question the
study behind it must have asked.\\[4pt]
\ansline{Answers vary. Look for: the individuals named, the variable and its units,}\\[1pt]\ansline{and a question whose answers differ across individuals rather than one value.}
\end{extensionbox}
