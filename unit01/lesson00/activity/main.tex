\boxguard[10]
\begin{headlinebox}{sky}
\small
\textbf{The Adoption Board.} A county animal shelter posts one board in its lobby listing every
dog currently up for adoption. Visitors read it, and so does the shelter director --- but they
are not looking for the same things. Work through both problems with your group, using the
vocabulary from today's notes. For \textbf{every} answer, be ready to point at the board and
show \emph{how you know}.
\end{headlinebox}

\vspace{0.10in}

\boxguard[30]
\begin{scenariobox}[1. Reading the Board]{navy}
\small
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.25}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{} l Y Y Y Y @{}}
\rowcolor{sky}
\textbf{Name} & \textbf{Breed} & \textbf{Age (yr)} & \textbf{Weight (lb)} & \textbf{Days on board} \\
\hline
Biscuit & Beagle    & 3 & 24 & 12 \\
Momo    & Terrier   & 1 & 15 & 44 \\
Ranger  & Shepherd  & 5 & 61 &  6 \\
Pepper  & Retriever & 2 & 55 & 21 \\
Sage    & Beagle    & 7 & 27 & 59 \\
Tuck    & Mixed     & 4 & 38 &  9 \\
Willow  & Terrier   & 2 & 17 & 33 \\
Zeke    & Shepherd  & 6 & 70 & 15 \\
\hline
\end{tabularx}

\vspace{6pt}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{\alph*.}, leftmargin=1.8em, itemsep=7pt]
  \item One \textbf{row} of this board describes one \blank{3.0cm}. The board lists
        \blank{1.0cm} of them, and records \blank{1.0cm} variables about each one
        (do not count the Name column).
  \item ``Beagle'' appears on the board. Is it a \textbf{variable} or a \textbf{value}?
        Circle one, then name the variable it belongs to.
        \hfill \textbf{variable} \qquad \textbf{value}
        \answerspace{1.2cm}{}
  \item \textbf{Underline} the part of the board you would read to answer each question. One
        needs a single \emph{value}; the other needs an entire \emph{variable}.

        \vspace{3pt}
        \emph{How much does Zeke weigh?} \qquad\qquad \emph{How much do these dogs weigh?}
        \answerspace{1.4cm}{}
  \item Sage has been on the board 59 days and Ranger 6. If \emph{every} dog had been on the
        board the same number of days, would the \emph{Days on board} column still be worth
        posting? Answer using the word \textbf{variability}.
        \answerspace{1.8cm}{}
\end{enumerate}
\end{scenariobox}

\vspace{0.10in}

\boxguard[26]
\begin{scenariobox}[2. Four Questions the Director Might Ask]{navy}
\small
The shelter director looks at the same board and asks these four questions.

\vspace{4pt}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{\alph*.}, leftmargin=1.8em, itemsep=7pt]
  \item For each one, decide whether you could answer it by reading \textbf{one spot} on the
        board or whether you would have to read \textbf{many rows}.

        \begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{(\Alph*)}, leftmargin=2.2em, itemsep=4pt]
          \item Is Momo a terrier? \hfill \blank{3.4cm}
          \item How many dogs are on the board? \hfill \blank{3.4cm}
          \item How long do dogs on this board tend to wait? \hfill \blank{3.4cm}
          \item Do heavier dogs tend to wait longer than lighter ones? \hfill \blank{3.4cm}
        \end{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Three of those need many rows. Two of the three are statistical questions and
        one is not.} Find the one that is not, then say exactly which check it fails and why.
        \answerspace{2.4cm}{}
  \item A flyer taped under the board reads: \textbf{``Our dogs go home in 25 days.''}
        Name \textbf{two} pieces of context the flyer leaves out that you would want before
        believing it.
        \answerspace{1.8cm}{}
  \item A visitor reads the flyer and says, ``Good --- so I'll have my dog home in 25 days.''
        Using specific values from the board, give one reason that is the wrong way to read
        the number.
        \answerspace{1.8cm}{}
\end{enumerate}
\end{scenariobox}

\vspace{0.10in}

% ── DEBRIEF (8 min, whole class — filled together after the groups report out) ─
\boxguard[18]
\begin{reflectionbox}
\small
\textbf{Debrief --- we fill this in together.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.9em, itemsep=8pt]
  \item In one sentence, what is the single thing that separates a statistical question from
        a question that is not statistical?
        \answerspace{1.2cm}{}
  \item Write a question about this board that needs the \textbf{whole table} and is still
        \textbf{not} statistical. Say which check it fails.
        \answerspace{1.4cm}{}
  \item Why would statistics be unnecessary if every dog on the board were identical?
        \answerspace{1.2cm}{}
\end{enumerate}
\end{reflectionbox}
