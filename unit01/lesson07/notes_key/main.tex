% ── ROW 1: parameter vs statistic ────────────────────────────────────────────
\mainidea[Parameter \&]{Statistic} &
A \textbf{parameter} is a numerical summary of a \ans{population} --- \emph{everybody}. Its symbols
are \ans{$\mu$} for the mean and \ans{$\sigma$} for the standard deviation.
\par\vspace{2pt}
A \textbf{statistic} is a numerical summary of a \ans{sample} --- \emph{some of them}. Its
symbols, from Lesson~1.5, are \ans{$\bar x$} and \ans{$s_x$}.
\par\vspace{3pt}
\textbf{The test:} ask \ans{who got measured} --- everyone, or a handful?

% ── ROW 2: the standardized score — two tryouts ──────────────────────────────
\mainidea[Standardized score]{$z$-score} &
One scholarship, two candidates, two different events. Maya threw 12.0 m; Dev ran 12.3 s.
\par\vspace{2pt}
A \textbf{standardized score}, or \textbf{$z$-score}, counts how many \ans{standard deviations} a value sits
from the mean of its \emph{own} distribution:
\par\vspace{3pt}
\centerline{$z = \dfrac{x_i - \mu}{\sigma}$}
\par\vspace{3pt}
\stepnum{1}\ Subtract the mean: $x_i - \mu$ is the distance in the variable's own
\ans{units}.\par
\stepnum{2}\ Divide by $\sigma$: the units \ans{cancel}, and what is left is a plain count.\par
\stepnum{3}\ Keep the \ans{sign}: positive means \ans{above} the mean, negative means
\ans{below} it.

% ── ROW 3: the normal distribution and the empirical rule ────────────────────
\mainidea[Normal distribution \&]{Empirical rule} &
A \textbf{normal distribution} is drawn as a curve that is \ans{symmetric} about its centre and
\ans{mound}-shaped, and it is fixed entirely by \ans{$\mu$} and \ans{$\sigma$}. Real data is
never exactly that shape, so we say a data set is \ans{approximately} normal.
\par\vspace{2pt}
\textbf{Empirical rule.} If a distribution is approximately normal, then about \ans{68\%} of
the observations fall within 1 standard deviation of the mean, about \ans{95\%} within 2, and
about \ans{99.7\%} within 3.
