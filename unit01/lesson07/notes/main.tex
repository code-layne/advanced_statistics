% ── ROW 2: the standardized score — two tryouts ──────────────────────────────
\mainidea[Standardized score]{$z$-score} &
One scholarship, two events: Maya threw 12.0 m; Dev ran 12.3 s. Nearly equal numbers, and not on
the same \blank{1.3cm}. A \textbf{standardized score}, or \textbf{$z$-score}, counts how many
\blank{3.4cm} a value sits from the mean of its \emph{own} distribution:
\par\vspace{2pt}
\centerline{$z = \dfrac{x_i - \mu}{\sigma}$}
\par\vspace{2pt}
\stepnum{1}\ Subtract the mean: a distance in the variable's own \blank{1.3cm}.\par
\stepnum{2}\ Divide by $\sigma$: the units \blank{1.4cm}, leaving a plain count.\par
\stepnum{3}\ Keep the \blank{1.0cm}: positive is \blank{1.3cm} the mean, negative \blank{1.3cm} it.

% ── ROW 3: the normal distribution and the empirical rule ────────────────────
\mainidea[Normal distribution \&]{Empirical rule} &
A \textbf{normal distribution} is a curve \blank{2.1cm} about its centre and
\blank{1.5cm}-shaped, fixed entirely by \blank{0.8cm} and \blank{0.8cm}. Real data is never
\emph{exactly} that, so we say a data set is \blank{2.8cm} normal.
\\

\vspace{2pt}
\textbf{Empirical rule.} If a distribution is approximately normal, about \blank{1.1cm} of
observations lie within 1 standard deviation of the mean, \blank{1.1cm} within 2, \blank{1.3cm}
within 3.
\\
& Two unrelated records --- a bakery's \textbf{500} loaf weights (mean 800 g, s.d.\ 20 g) and a
clinic's \textbf{400} body temperatures (mean 98.2$^\circ$F, s.d.\ 0.7$^\circ$F) --- each sorted
into bands one standard deviation wide. One loaf and one reading fell outside.
\par\vspace{2pt}

\textbf{Band} & $-3$ to $-2$ & $-2$ to $-1$ & $-1$ to 0 & 0 to $+1$ & $+1$ to $+2$ &
  $+2$ to $+3$ \\
\hline
Loaves (500) & 11 & 68 & 170 & 171 & 67 & 12 \\
Readings (400) & 9 & 54 & 137 & 136 & 54 & 9 \\
\end{tabularx}\small
\par\vspace{2pt}
Add the loaves' bands outward:
\begin{work}
\text{within } 1\sigma &: 170 + 171 = 341 \to \tfrac{341}{500} = 68.2\% \\
\text{within } 2\sigma &: 341 + 68 + 67 = 476 \to \tfrac{476}{500} = 95.2\% \\
\text{within } 3\sigma &: 476 + 11 + 12 = 499 \to \tfrac{499}{500} = 99.8\%
\end{work}

% ── ROW 4: THE CRUX — the raw-number comparison, and the sign read wrongly ───
\mainidea[Whose day was]{Better?} &
\textbf{A coach settles the scholarship in one line: ``Dev ran 12.3 and Maya threw 12.0, so Dev's

\fcolorbox{navy}{sky}{\parbox{\dimexpr\linewidth-2\fboxsep-2\fboxrule\relax}{\centering
\textbf{A raw value cannot be compared across two different \blank{2.8cm}.}\par
Standardize each against its own mean and standard deviation, then read the
\textbf{\blank{2.0cm}} in context.}}
\\
& The sign is not a verdict: it says which \blank{1.0cm} of the mean the value fell on. A negative

average on a 100 m \emph{time} means \blank{1.5cm} than the field.
\par\vspace{2pt}
Likewise the empirical rule is a fact about a \blank{1.3cm}: a strongly \blank{1.4cm} or

\mainidea[Guided practice]{The Bread Loaf} &
The bakery as a \emph{model}: loaf weights are approximately normal, $\mu = 800$ g and
$\sigma = 20$ g. A loaf comes off the line at 834 g:
\begin{work}
z &= \tfrac{834 - 800}{20} = \tfrac{34}{20} = 1.7
\end{work}
That loaf is 1.7 standard deviations \blank{1.3cm} the mean weight of \blank{1.1cm} grams.
\\
& \textbf{A tail.} About 95\% lie within 2 standard deviations, so about \blank{1.0cm} lie
outside; the curve is \blank{2.1cm}, so that splits evenly:
\begin{work}
840 \text{ g} &\to z = +2, \quad 5\% \text{ outside} \to \tfrac{5\%}{2} = 2.5\% \text{ above}
\end{work}
\\
& \textbf{A complaint.} A customer returns a 754 g loaf as ``way underweight'':
\begin{work}
z &= \tfrac{754 - 800}{20} = \tfrac{-46}{20} = -2.3
\end{work}
Fewer than \blank{1.3cm} of loaves are that light: the complaint has \blank{1.4cm}. But the
model is only \blank{2.8cm} normal, and one loaf says nothing about the \blank{1.1cm}.
\\
