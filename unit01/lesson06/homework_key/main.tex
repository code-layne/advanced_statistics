\begin{notesbox}{Reading, Correcting, and Comparing Boxplots}
Show the arithmetic wherever a question asks for a number, and write every explanation
\emph{in the context of the problem} --- that is what earns credit on the AP exam, and it is the
standard here.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.9em, itemsep=8pt]

  \item Eleven students reported how many minutes they spent reading last night. The values are
        already in order.

        \vspace{3pt}
        \begin{center}
        4 \quad 6 \quad 7 \quad 9 \quad 12 \quad 15 \quad 16 \quad 18 \quad 19 \quad 26 \quad 34
        \end{center}
        \vspace{3pt}

        \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.3}
        \begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{} Y Y Y Y Y @{}}
        \rowcolor{sky}
        \textbf{Minimum} & \textbf{$Q_1$} & \textbf{Median} & \textbf{$Q_3$} &
        \textbf{Maximum} \\
        \hline
        \ans{4} & \ans{7} & \ans{15} & \ans{19} & \ans{34} \\
        \end{tabularx}

        \vspace{6pt}
        Exactly one of these three boxplots was built from your five numbers. Which one?

        \vspace{4pt}
        \begin{center}
        \begin{tikzpicture}[scale=0.9]
          \foreach \x in {0,0.95,1.9,2.85,3.8,4.75,5.7,6.65} {
            \draw[linegray, very thin] (\x,-0.30) -- (\x,2.85);}
          \bxp{2.4}{0.28}{0.76}{1.33}{2.09}{3.61}{6.46}
          \bxp{1.4}{0.28}{0.76}{1.33}{2.85}{3.61}{6.46}
          \bxp{0.4}{0.28}{0.76}{2.28}{2.85}{4.94}{6.46}
          \node[font=\bfseries\scriptsize, anchor=east] at (-0.20,2.4) {Plot A};
          \node[font=\bfseries\scriptsize, anchor=east] at (-0.20,1.4) {Plot B};
          \node[font=\bfseries\scriptsize, anchor=east] at (-0.20,0.4) {Plot C};
          \draw[->, thick] (-0.15,-0.30) -- (7.10,-0.30);
          \foreach \x/\lab in {0/0, 0.95/5, 1.9/10, 2.85/15, 3.8/20, 4.75/25, 5.7/30,
                               6.65/35} {
            \draw (\x,-0.38) -- (\x,-0.22); \node[below, font=\scriptsize] at (\x,-0.42) {\lab};}
          \node[font=\scriptsize] at (3.3,-0.98) {Minutes spent reading};
        \end{tikzpicture}
        \end{center}
        \vspace{2pt}

        Plot: \ans{B} \quad What is wrong with each of the other two?
        \answerspace{2.2cm}{Plot A puts the inside line near 11 instead of 15. Plot C runs its box
        from about 12 to 26 instead of 7 to 19, so both quartiles are wrong.}

  \item A company made a boxplot of the commute times of its 100 employees. The left whisker is
        very short and the right whisker is very long. A student writes: \emph{``the long right
        whisker means most of the commute times are large.''} Correct that student.
        \answerspace{2.4cm}{The right whisker holds one quarter of the employees --- 25 of the 100
        --- exactly as many as the short left whisker. Its length shows those 25 times are spread
        far apart, not that there are more of them. Half of all 100 are below the median line.}

  \item A city recorded how many minutes each of 40 late buses was delayed. For those 40 delays,
        $Q_1 = 18$ minutes and $Q_3 = 30$ minutes. One bus was delayed \textbf{52} minutes. Use
        the $1.5 \times \text{IQR}$ rule to decide whether that delay is an outlier.

        \begin{work}
        \text{IQR} &= 30 - 18 = 12 \\
        1.5 \times \text{IQR} &= 1.5 \times 12 = 18 \\
        \text{upper boundary} &= Q_3 + 18 = 30 + 18 = 48 \\
        52 &> 48 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad \text{yes, 52 minutes is an outlier}
        \end{work}

        On a boxplot of these delays, how would the 52-minute bus be drawn, and where would the
        right whisker stop?
        \answerspace{2.2cm}{As its own point at 52 minutes, past the end of the whisker. The
        whisker stops at the longest delay that is 48 minutes or less --- the most extreme delay
        that is not an outlier.}

  \item Two pizza shops recorded their delivery times over one month.

        \vspace{4pt}
        \begin{center}
        \begin{tikzpicture}[scale=0.9]
          \foreach \x in {0,0.95,1.9,2.85,3.8,4.75,5.7,6.65} {
            \draw[linegray, very thin] (\x,-0.30) -- (\x,1.85);}
          \bxp{1.3}{0.30}{0.76}{1.71}{2.28}{2.85}{3.99}
          \bxp{0.4}{0.30}{0.38}{1.33}{3.04}{4.56}{6.46}
          \node[font=\bfseries\scriptsize, anchor=east] at (-0.20,1.3) {Shop A};
          \node[font=\bfseries\scriptsize, anchor=east] at (-0.20,0.4) {Shop B};
          \draw[->, thick] (-0.15,-0.30) -- (7.10,-0.30);
          \foreach \x/\lab in {0/10, 0.95/15, 1.9/20, 2.85/25, 3.8/30, 4.75/35, 5.7/40,
                               6.65/45} {
            \draw (\x,-0.38) -- (\x,-0.22); \node[below, font=\scriptsize] at (\x,-0.42) {\lab};}
          \node[font=\scriptsize] at (3.3,-0.98) {Delivery time (minutes)};
        \end{tikzpicture}
        \end{center}
        \vspace{2pt}

        Compare the two shops' delivery times in one paragraph. Cover center, variability, and
        unusual features, in context, using comparative language --- then say which shop you
        would order from when you are in a hurry.
        \answerspace{2.6cm}{Shop A's delivery times were typically shorter than Shop B's --- median
        22 minutes against 26 --- and far more consistent: its middle half spans 6 minutes
        against 17, and its range is 17 against 32. Neither shows an outlier, though Shop B
        trails right. In a hurry, order from Shop A.}

  \item \textbf{AP-style multiple choice.} A boxplot summarizes the commute times, in minutes,
        of 200 employees. The five-number summary is 8, 21, 27, 46, and 95 minutes. Which
        statement \textbf{must} be true? Circle one, then justify it in a sentence.
        \begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{(\Alph*)}, leftmargin=2.2em, itemsep=2pt]
          \item Most of the commute times are between 46 and 95 minutes, because that is the
                widest section of the plot.
          \item \ans{About 50 of the 200 commute times are between 27 and 46 minutes.}
          \item The commute times have two peaks, one near 21 minutes and one near 46 minutes.
          \item The mean commute time is 27 minutes.
          \item No employee commuted for longer than 46 minutes.
        \end{enumerate}
        \answerspace{2.2cm}{(B). The stretch from the median to $Q_3$ holds one quarter of the 200
        commutes, which is 50 --- and every section holds a quarter, however wide it looks.}

\end{enumerate}
\end{notesbox}
