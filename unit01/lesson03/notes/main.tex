By the end of these notes I will be able to:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em, nosep, after=\vspace{2pt}]

% ── I DO: section 1 ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\boxguard[20]
\begin{notesbox}{1. Two Kinds of Quantitative Variable}
\small
Last lesson every column we summarized was categorical, and we counted it. Today's columns hold
\emph{numbers}, and numbers come in two kinds.

\vspace{5pt}
A \textbf{discrete} variable takes a \blank{2.0cm} number of values --- you can list them, and
nothing sits between two neighbours. A \textbf{continuous} variable takes \blank{2.2cm} many:
between any two values there is always \blank{2.4cm}.

\vspace{5pt}
\textbf{The test is the one from the warm-up.} Ask whether a value like 7.5 makes sense.
A teacher records two things about the same quiz: how many questions each student attempted,
and how many minutes each student took.

\vspace{4pt}
\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.35}
\begin{tabularx}{0.92\linewidth}{@{} l Y Y @{}}
\rowcolor{sky}
\textbf{Variable} & \textbf{Counted or measured?} & \textbf{Discrete or continuous?} \\
\hline
Questions attempted & counted  & \blank{2.2cm} \\
Minutes to finish   & measured & \blank{2.2cm} \\
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

\vspace{5pt}
\begin{center}
\fcolorbox{redacc}{redbg}{\parbox{0.94\linewidth}{\small
\textbf{Careful --- the recorded digits are not the variable.} A rain gauge reports 13 mm and
never 13.4 mm, but rainfall is still \emph{continuous}: the rain that fell was some real amount,
and the gauge rounded it. Ask about the \blank{1.8cm}, not about the digits somebody wrote down.
\emph{Probe:} \emph{could a better instrument have split that value?}}}
\end{center}
\end{notesbox}

\vspace{0.10in}

% ── I DO: section 2 — displays that keep every value ─────────────────────────
\boxguard[30]
\begin{notesbox}{2. The Class Record: Displays That Keep Every Value}
\small
Twenty-four students took that 50-minute quiz. Here is how the teacher first wrote down how long
each one took. The digit left of the line is the \emph{tens} digit; each digit right of it is a
\emph{ones} digit, so the row marked \textbf{3} holds 34, 35, 36, 36, 38, and 39.

\vspace{4pt}
\begin{minipage}[t]{0.34\linewidth}
\vspace{0pt}
\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.2}
\begin{tabular}{@{} r | l @{}}
\textbf{1} & 1 \ 2 \ 2 \ 4 \ 5 \ 5 \ 6 \ 7 \ 8 \ 8 \ 9 \\
\textbf{2} & 0 \\
\textbf{3} & 4 \ 5 \ 6 \ 6 \ 8 \ 9 \\
\textbf{4} & 0 \ 1 \ 2 \ 3 \ 4 \ 6 \\
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\end{minipage}
\hfill
\begin{minipage}[t]{0.62\linewidth}
\vspace{0pt}
\begin{center}
\begin{tikzpicture}[scale=0.86]
  \draw[->, thick] (-0.25,0) -- (8.4,0);
  \foreach \x/\lab in {0/10, 2/20, 4/30, 6/40, 8/50} {
    \draw (\x,-0.07) -- (\x,0.07); \node[below, font=\tiny] at (\x,-0.1) {\lab};}
  \foreach \v/\n in {11/1, 12/2, 14/1, 15/2, 16/1, 17/1, 18/2, 19/1, 20/1,
                     34/1, 35/1, 36/2, 38/1, 39/1, 40/1, 41/1, 42/1, 43/1, 44/1, 46/1} {
    \foreach \k in {1,...,\n} {
      \fill[navy] ({(\v-10)*0.2},{0.155*\k}) circle (0.052);}}
  \node[font=\tiny] at (4.0,-0.52) {Minutes to finish};
\end{tikzpicture}
\end{center}
\end{minipage}

\vspace{6pt}
\textbf{Read them.} Students shown: \blank{1.2cm} \quad Fastest: \blank{1.5cm} \quad
Slowest: \blank{1.5cm}\\[4pt]
How many students took exactly 36 minutes? \blank{1.2cm}

\vspace{6pt}
On the left is a \textbf{stem plot}: each value is split into a \blank{1.3cm} (the leading
digits) and a \blank{1.3cm} (the final digit). On the right is a \textbf{dotplot}: one
\blank{1.1cm} per observation, stacked above its value on a number line.

\vspace{4pt}
The two displays look nothing alike and carry exactly the same information, because both keep
\blank{2.4cm} individual value. That is why you can still find the two 36s in either one.
\end{notesbox}

\vspace{0.10in}

% ── I DO: section 3 — grouping ───────────────────────────────────────────────
\boxguard[30]
\begin{notesbox}{3. Grouping: From the Record to a Histogram}
\small
Twenty-four dots is readable. Two hundred would not be. So we \textbf{group}. Count the times
off the plots above into five-minute blocks:

\vspace{4pt}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.25}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{} l Y Y Y Y Y Y Y Y @{}}
\rowcolor{sky}
\textbf{Minutes} & 10--14 & 15--19 & 20--24 & 25--29 & 30--34 & 35--39 & 40--44 & 45--49 \\
\hline
\textbf{Students} & \blank{0.9cm} & \blank{0.9cm} & \blank{0.9cm} & \blank{0.9cm} &
\blank{0.9cm} & \blank{0.9cm} & \blank{0.9cm} & \blank{0.9cm} \\
\end{tabularx}

\vspace{6pt}
\textbf{That table is already the histogram.} A \textbf{histogram} draws each interval's count
as a bar, so the height of a bar is the number of observations falling in that \blank{1.8cm}.
What you give up is the individual values: after grouping, they are \blank{1.2cm}. Nothing in
the picture below can tell you that two students took exactly 36 minutes.

\vspace{2pt}
\begin{center}
\begin{tikzpicture}[scale=0.74]
  \foreach \y/\lab in {0/0, 1.4/5, 2.8/10} {
    \draw[linegray, very thin] (0,\y) -- (5.6,\y);
    \draw (-0.08,\y) -- (0.08,\y) node[left, font=\tiny, xshift=-2pt] {\lab};}
  \foreach \i/\c in {0/4, 1/7, 2/1, 3/0, 4/1, 5/5, 6/5, 7/1} {
    \fill[navy] ({\i*0.7},0) rectangle ({(\i+1)*0.7},{\c*0.28});
    \draw[white, very thin] ({\i*0.7},0) rectangle ({(\i+1)*0.7},{\c*0.28});}
  \draw[->, thick] (0,0) -- (0,3.9);
  \draw[->, thick] (0,0) -- (6.0,0);
  \foreach \x/\lab in {0/10, 1.4/20, 2.8/30, 4.2/40, 5.6/50} {
    \draw (\x,-0.08) -- (\x,0.08); \node[below, font=\tiny] at (\x,-0.12) {\lab};}
  \node[font=\tiny] at (2.8,-0.62) {Minutes to finish};
  \node[rotate=90, font=\tiny] at (-0.72,1.6) {Students};
\end{tikzpicture}
\end{center}

\vspace{2pt}
\textbf{The bars touch}, and last lesson's bar-graph bars did not. The difference is the
horizontal axis: here it is a \blank{2.4cm}, so the intervals are next to each other with
nothing between them. A bar graph's axis held separate category names, so those bars needed
\blank{1.3cm}.

\vspace{5pt}
\textbf{One more display, named now and used later.} A \textbf{cumulative graph} answers a
different question about the same 24 times: how many students finished \blank{2.6cm} a given
number of minutes. It returns in Lesson 1.6 with percentiles.
\end{notesbox}

% ── I DO: section 4 — the trap ───────────────────────────────────────────────
\boxguard[30]
\begin{notesbox}{4. The Same 24 Times, Drawn Twice}
\small
Both pictures below were built from the \emph{same} 24 finishing times. Nothing was left out of
either one.

\vspace{4pt}
\begin{center}
\begin{tikzpicture}[scale=0.68]
  % ── left: five-minute blocks ──
  \foreach \y/\lab in {0/0, 1.4/5, 2.8/10} {
    \draw[linegray, very thin] (0,\y) -- (5.6,\y);
    \draw (-0.08,\y) -- (0.08,\y) node[left, font=\tiny, xshift=-2pt] {\lab};}
  \foreach \i/\c in {0/4, 1/7, 2/1, 3/0, 4/1, 5/5, 6/5, 7/1} {
    \fill[navy] ({\i*0.7},0) rectangle ({(\i+1)*0.7},{\c*0.28});
    \draw[white, very thin] ({\i*0.7},0) rectangle ({(\i+1)*0.7},{\c*0.28});}
  \draw[->, thick] (0,0) -- (0,4.0);
  \draw[->, thick] (0,0) -- (6.0,0);
  \foreach \x/\lab in {0/10, 1.4/20, 2.8/30, 4.2/40, 5.6/50} {
    \draw (\x,-0.08) -- (\x,0.08); \node[below, font=\tiny] at (\x,-0.12) {\lab};}
  \node[font=\tiny] at (2.8,-0.62) {Minutes to finish};
  \node[font=\bfseries\scriptsize] at (2.8,4.3) {Blocks of 5 minutes};
  % ── right: twenty-minute blocks ──
  \begin{scope}[xshift=8.0cm]
    \foreach \y/\lab in {0/0, 1.4/5, 2.8/10} {
      \draw[linegray, very thin] (0,\y) -- (5.6,\y);
      \draw (-0.08,\y) -- (0.08,\y) node[left, font=\tiny, xshift=-2pt] {\lab};}
    \foreach \i/\c in {0/12, 1/12} {
      \fill[goldacc] ({\i*2.8},0) rectangle ({(\i+1)*2.8},{\c*0.28});
      \draw[white, very thin] ({\i*2.8},0) rectangle ({(\i+1)*2.8},{\c*0.28});}
    \draw[->, thick] (0,0) -- (0,4.0);
    \draw[->, thick] (0,0) -- (6.0,0);
    \foreach \x/\lab in {0/10, 2.8/30, 5.6/50} {
      \draw (\x,-0.08) -- (\x,0.08); \node[below, font=\tiny] at (\x,-0.12) {\lab};}
    \node[font=\tiny] at (2.8,-0.62) {Minutes to finish};
    \node[font=\bfseries\scriptsize] at (2.8,4.3) {Blocks of 20 minutes};
  \end{scope}
\end{tikzpicture}
\end{center}

\vspace{4pt}
\textbf{Before arguing about them, check that neither one dropped anybody.} Add the bar heights:

\begin{work}
\text{Blocks of 5 minutes} &= 4+7+1+0+1+5+5+1 = 24 \\[4pt]
\text{Blocks of 20 minutes} &= 12 + 12 = 24
\end{work}

Both come to \blank{1.1cm}, so no data went missing either way. The left picture says the class
split into a fast group and a slow group with almost nobody in between; the right picture says
students finished \blank{2.4cm} across the period. The only thing that was changed is the
\blank{2.6cm}.

\vspace{6pt}
\begin{center}
\fcolorbox{navy}{sky}{\parbox{0.92\linewidth}{\centering\small
\textbf{Changing the interval width changes the \blank{2.2cm} of a histogram}\\
--- without changing a single data value.}}
\end{center}

\vspace{6pt}
\textbf{So neither picture is wrong, and they are not equally useful.} The teacher who wants to
know whether the quiz was the right length needs the \blank{2.0cm} picture, because the gap she
has to see is the thing the wider blocks average away.

\vspace{4pt}
\textbf{Narrower is not automatically better either.} Redraw these 24 times in blocks of one
minute and almost every bar is \blank{1.6cm} tall, because the values barely repeat: you get
every value back and no shape at all. Too wide \blank{1.4cm} features; too narrow shows
\blank{1.4cm}. \emph{A histogram is a choice, not a fact} --- so before you believe a shape,
check what width produced it.
\end{notesbox}

\begin{practicebox}
\small
\textbf{The Bus Stop --- we work this one together.} Here are the waiting times, in minutes, for
18 riders at one bus stop on a weekday morning.

\vspace{3pt}
\begin{center}
\begin{tikzpicture}[scale=0.86]
  \draw[->, thick] (-0.2,0) -- (7.4,0);
  \foreach \x/\lab in {0/0, 1/2, 2/4, 3/6, 4/8, 5/10, 6/12, 7/14} {
    \draw (\x,-0.07) -- (\x,0.07); \node[below, font=\tiny] at (\x,-0.1) {\lab};}
  \foreach \x/\n in {0.5/2, 1/3, 1.5/4, 2/3, 2.5/2, 3/1, 5.5/1, 6/2} {
    \foreach \k in {1,...,\n} { \fill[navy] (\x,{0.2*\k}) circle (0.07); }}
  \node[font=\tiny] at (3.6,-0.52) {Minutes waited};
\end{tikzpicture}
\end{center}

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{\alph*.}, leftmargin=1.8em, itemsep=7pt]
  \item How many riders are shown? \blank{1.5cm} \quad How many waited longer than 8 minutes?
        \blank{2.6cm} \quad Which display is this? \blank{1.8cm}
  \item Is waiting time discrete or continuous? \blank{2.2cm} \quad Say how you can tell from
        the variable itself rather than from the picture.\\[3pt]
        \writelines{2}
  \item \textbf{Redraw it.} Suppose these 18 times were grouped into five-minute blocks instead.
        What happens to the two riders out at 11 and 12 minutes, and would the new picture still
        show that they stand apart from everyone else?\\[3pt]
        \writelines{2}
\end{enumerate}
\end{practicebox}

\begin{notesbox}{Independent Practice --- You Try}
\small
Work these on your own. I will circulate.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.9em, itemsep=8pt]
  \item A librarian records how many days each of 120 borrowed books was kept. Drawn in
        \textbf{one-day} blocks the histogram is ragged --- nearly every bar is 1 or 2 books
        tall. Drawn in \textbf{seven-day} blocks it shows three clean bars.

        \vspace{3pt}
        A classmate says the one-day version must be wrong, because a histogram should not look
        that ragged. Do you agree? Say which version you would hand someone who wants the
        overall pattern, and why.\\[3pt]
        \writelines{2}
  \item A veterinary form records the \textbf{number of pets} in a household and the
        \textbf{pet's weight, rounded to the nearest kilogram}.

        \vspace{3pt}
        Number of pets: \blank{2.2cm} \quad Weight in kilograms: \blank{2.4cm}

        \vspace{3pt}
        A classmate says weight has to be discrete here, because every recorded weight is a
        whole number. Answer him.\\[3pt]
        \writelines{2}
  \item A histogram of 40 quiz scores has a bar of height 3 over the interval 85--89. A student
        writes, ``Three students scored 87.'' Say what the histogram actually supports, and name
        a display that would let you check his claim.\\[3pt]
        \writelines{2}
\end{enumerate}
\end{notesbox}
