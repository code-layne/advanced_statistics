% ── ROW 1: center and variability — the two checkout lines ───────────────────
\mainidea[Center \&]{Variability} &
The \textbf{center} of a distribution is its middle, typical value. The \textbf{variability}, or
\textbf{spread}, is how far the values reach from each other. Today we describe both by eye.

\vspace{3pt}

% ── ROW 2: shape — count the peaks, then check the mirror ────────────────────
\mainidea[Shape]{Peaks \& Mirror} &
\textbf{Count the peaks first.} One main peak is \textbf{unimodal}; two clear peaks is
\textbf{bimodal}; all bars about level is \textbf{uniform}. \textbf{Then check the mirror:}
a distribution is \textbf{symmetric} when its left half roughly mirrors its right half.

\vspace{3pt}

% ── ROW 3: unusual features and the four-part description ────────────────────
\mainidea[Unusual]{Features} &
An \textbf{outlier} is a value that sits unusually far from the rest of the data. A
\textbf{gap} is a stretch of the number line where nothing was observed. A \textbf{cluster} is
a group of values bunched together and set off from the rest by a gap.
