\begin{vocabbox}
\small
Fill in each term \textbf{as we name it} in the notes below.
\par
\termans{Categorical variable}{takes values that are category names or group labels --- Gold, Cedar Falls, Yes.}
\par
\termans{Quantitative variable}{takes numerical values for a measured or counted amount --- minutes, pounds, bottles.}
\par
\termans{The amount test}{ask whether the value records how much or how many, not whether it is written in digits.}
\par
\termans{Grouping (binning)}{replacing measured amounts with group labels; turns Q into C, and the mean cannot be recovered.}

\begin{guidednotes}
% ── ROW 1: two kinds of variable ─────────────────────────────────────────────
\mainidea[Two kinds of]{Variable} &
A \textbf{variable} is any characteristic recorded for each individual; today we split them in
two. A \textbf{categorical} variable's values are category \ans{names} or group labels. A
\textbf{quantitative} variable's values are \ans{numerical} for a measured or counted amount.

\vspace{3pt}
Three hiking parties, two kinds of column:
\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.1}
\begin{tabular}{@{} l l c @{}}
\rowcolor{sky}
\textbf{Party} & \textbf{Trail} & \textbf{Time out (min)} \\
\hline
Alvarez & Ridge Loop   &  95 \\
Boone   & Cedar Falls  & 140 \\
Delgado & Summit Trail & 215 \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\emph{Trail} is \ans{categorical}: its values name which trail, and Summit Trail is not ``more''
than Ridge Loop. \emph{Time out} is \ans{quantitative}: 215 minutes really is more than 95, and the
difference, 120 minutes, means something.
\\
& \notesprompt{The ranger records more about each party. Classify each: C or Q.}
\begin{probgrid}{|Y|Y|Y|}
\pcell{1}{Group size (number of hikers)}{0.9cm}{Q} &
\pcell{2}{Dog along (Yes / No)}{0.9cm}{C} &
\pcell{3}{Weather at the trailhead}{0.9cm}{C} \\ \hline
\pcell{4}{Pack weight, in pounds}{1.1cm}{Q} &
\pcell{5}{Trail difficulty (Easy / Moderate / Hard)}{1.1cm}{C --- labels, not amounts} &
\pcell{6}{Miles hiked}{1.1cm}{Q} \\ \hline
\end{probgrid}
\\ \hline
% ── ROW 2: the amount test ───────────────────────────────────────────────────
\mainidea[The]{Amount test} &
Do not ask ``does it have digits.'' Ask: \textbf{is the value an \emph{amount} of something ---
how much, or how many?} If yes, the variable is \ans{quantitative}. If the value only
\emph{identifies} or \emph{labels}, it is \ans{categorical}.

\vspace{3pt}
A second way to run the same test: \textbf{does averaging the values produce something real?}
If the average is \ans{nonsense}, the variable is categorical no matter how it is written.

\\
& Two hiking parties hold permits 2041 and 6318 and carry 6 and 2 water bottles. Both columns are
written entirely in digits. Both averages compute without complaint:

\begin{work}
\text{average permit \#} &= \frac{2041 + 6318}{2} = 4179.5 \\[4pt]
\text{average water bottles} &= \frac{6 + 2}{2} = 4
\end{work}
\notesprompt{Run the test on both averages.}
\begin{probgrid}{|Y|Y|Y|}
\pcell{7}{Four water bottles --- what real thing does that number describe?}{1.5cm}{An amount: bottles per party, if shared evenly} &
\pcell{8}{Hand me the party whose permit is 4179.5.}{1.5cm}{Nobody --- the digits are a label, not an amount} &
\pcell{9}{Which column passes the amount test, and what is it an amount of?}{1.5cm}{Water bottles: an amount of bottles (how many)} \\ \hline
\end{probgrid}
\\ \hline
% ── ROW 3: grouping runs one way ─────────────────────────────────────────────
\mainidea[Grouping is]{One way only} &
A ranger stops recording \emph{Time out} in minutes and records \emph{Short} / \emph{Medium} /
\emph{Long} instead. Same hikes, same afternoon:

\vspace{2pt}
\centerline{\emph{Time out} \ 95, 140, 215 \ minutes \quad$\longrightarrow$\quad
\emph{Length} \ Short, Medium, Long}

\vspace{2pt}
The new column is \ans{categorical}. Replacing measured amounts with group labels is called
\textbf{grouping} or \textbf{binning}. Once 95, 140, and 215 become labels, the mean time on the
trail can no longer be \ans{computed} from the column. Grouping runs \ans{one} way only:
you can always bin amounts into labels, but you can never \ans{recover} the amounts from the
labels.
\\
& \notesprompt{Use the two columns.}
\begin{probgrid}{|Y|Y|}
\pcell{10}{Which column can be averaged? Compute that average.}{1.3cm}{Time out: $(95+140+215)/3 = 150$ minutes} &
\pcell{11}{Boone's row now reads \emph{Medium}. Can the ranger get 140 back from it? Why?}{1.3cm}{No --- \emph{Medium} could be any of many times} \\ \hline
\pcell{12}{Name one thing the ranger can no longer compute from the new column.}{1.2cm}{The mean time out (or which hike was longest)} &
\pcell{13}{A second ranger records \emph{Under 2 hours} / \emph{2 hours or more}. Which kind of
variable is that column?}{1.2cm}{Categorical --- two group labels} \\ \hline
\end{probgrid}
\\ \hline
% ── ROW 4: digits are not amounts — THE CRUX ─────────────────────────────────
\mainidea[Digits are not]{Amounts} &
A column written entirely in digits is \ans{not} automatically quantitative. Permit numbers
and ZIP codes average without complaint and mean \ans{nothing}: their digits \ans{identify} an
individual, they do not measure anything.

\vspace{3pt}
Counting a categorical variable also produces numbers. ``62\% of parties hiked with a dog'' is a
numerical \ans{summary} of the categorical variable \emph{Dog along}. The \emph{summary} is
numeric; the \emph{variable} is still \ans{categorical}.
\\
& \notesprompt{Classify each. Write C or Q.}
\begin{probgrid}{|Y|Y|Y|Y|Y|}
\pcell{14}{Permit \#}{0.9cm}{C} &
\pcell{15}{Parking spot}{0.9cm}{C} &
\pcell{16}{Home ZIP}{0.9cm}{C} &
\pcell{17}{Water bottles}{0.9cm}{Q} &
\pcell{18}{Group size}{0.9cm}{Q} \\ \hline
\end{probgrid}
\notesprompt{Then say why.}
\begin{probgrid}{|Y|Y|}
\pcell{19}{Every entry in the \emph{Dog along} column is Yes or No. Where did the 62 come from
--- and is it a value of the variable?}{1.4cm}{Counted from the Yes entries; a summary, not a value} &
\pcell{20}{State the amount test in one sentence --- without using the word
\emph{digits}.}{1.4cm}{Is the value how much or how many of something?} \\ \hline
\end{probgrid}
\\ \hline
% ── ROW 5: guided practice — WE DO, together, fresh context ──────────────────
\mainidea[Guided practice]{The Season Pass} &
A ski area records five things about each of its 3{,}400 season-pass holders: \textbf{pass
number}, \textbf{age} (years), \textbf{pass tier} (Gold / Silver / Bronze), \textbf{miles
traveled} to the mountain, and \textbf{locker number}.

\vspace{4pt}
\textbf{21.} The individuals are \ans{the 3{,}400 pass holders}.

\vspace{4pt}
\textbf{22.} Label each variable \textbf{C} or \textbf{Q}:

\vspace{2pt}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.3}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{} Y Y Y Y Y @{}}
\textbf{Pass \#} & \textbf{Age} & \textbf{Pass tier} & \textbf{Miles traveled} &
\textbf{Locker \#} \\
\ans{C} & \ans{Q} & \ans{C} & \ans{Q} & \ans{C} \\
\end{tabularx}
\\
& \notesprompt{We work these together.}
\begin{probgrid}{|Y|Y|Y|}
\pcell{23}{\emph{Miles traveled} and \emph{Locker \#} are both digits. Say the difference in
one sentence.}{1.6cm}{Miles is how far; a locker number is a name} &
\pcell{24}{The resort stops recording age in years and records \textbf{age group} instead
(Under 18 / 18--34 / 35--54 / 55+). Which kind is the variable now, and what can the resort no
longer compute?}{1.6cm}{Categorical; the mean age is lost} &
\pcell{25}{A report states: \textbf{``The average pass number is 5{,}120.''} Why does that
number tell you nothing about the pass holders?}{1.6cm}{A pass number is a name, not an amount} \\ \hline
\end{probgrid}
